ROLE: You are an adversarial reviewer of a physics-first rendering project. Your job is to attack the design claims below, not to approve them. Do not be agreeable; a flaw found now is worth more than politeness. If a claim is wrong, say why physically. If a claim is right, say what would make it fail.

CLAIM UNDER TEST: "The decomposition in design draft v4-3.1 (stage 3 of v4 — the water volume itself) is the right shape: four blocks — (3.1) a gradient-index (GRIN) transport core whose first field is freshwater thermal schlieren, (3.2) an inhomogeneous radiative-transfer block (spatial fields of absorption / scattering / CDOM, per-path optical depth, a spatially variant halo kernel, and a path-radiance term), verified against a delta-tracking Monte Carlo reference that also measures where multiple scattering becomes first-order, (3.3) a two-layer density lane for brackish scenes only (salt wedge + river-plume fronts; two-layer shallow-water dynamics with an entrainment closure), and (3.4) field time evolution plus integrated verification. Nothing essential is missing, and nothing in it violates the project principle."

THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE (non-negotiable): reproduce nature, do not approximate its look. A method is admissible only if (a) it is neutral numerics — it changes only the error form of the same computation — or (b) it corresponds to actual physics. "It looks natural" is never a justification.

WHAT THE PROJECT IS: A physically based caustics / water-ripple filter. Pipeline: environment → water state (simulation) → light transport → image. Stage 1 = the water surface (complete). Stage 2 = bed and 3D objects. Stage 3 (this design) = the water volume itself: what happens to light between the surface and the bed, and on the way back. Stage 4 = camera / lens; the current view is a fixed top-down one. The human eye is a separate project (PJ9); rain is a separate project (PJ10); bubbles and splash as phenomena belong to stage 2 (stage 3 only consumes a concentration field if stage 2 supplies one).

WHAT IS ALREADY SETTLED — MEASURED OR RULED; DO NOT RE-LITIGATE:
- The existing homogeneous-medium stack is measurement-verified, not self-compared: the spectral split of absorption a(λ) and scattering b(λ) (v3.6); the Fournier–Forand phase function with an FF-CDF halo kernel (v16), which scored 5/5 against published phase-function data and replaced a refuted Henyey–Greenstein g=0.85; the deposit is an area-conserving mesh projection with sun-disc angular spread by disc quadrature (v4-1.8 / v4-1.9), proven at the main verification size. The phase function's shape, the deposit's area conservation, and the disc quadrature are NOT reopened by this design.
- Stage 3 carries GRIN schlieren transport and inhomogeneous RTE side by side (four-way rally ruling, corrected form): the earlier claim that schlieren is the only volume effect that changes image formation was struck — spatially varying absorption / scattering (particles, CDOM) changes image formation too. The draft extends this ruling; do not re-argue it.
- The density (salt) lane is gated to brackish scenes only (v5 salinity ruling: salinity is a real variable only there; in freshwater scenes it is forbidden as a variable). Inside the density family, river-plume fronts are the priority case.
- Freshwater ponds are density-physics scenes too: thermal stratification, cool-skin convection cells and springs ordinarily produce temperature differences of several K, and thermal schlieren is a pond phenomenon. Water temperature is already adopted in this project as the strongest single environmental factor. The draft extends this ruling by connecting the thermal lane to the GRIN block; the ruling itself is settled.
- The dynamics of convection itself (CFD-class solution of the convection PDE) and resolved Kelvin–Helmholtz billows are out of scope by ruling: only their products are modelled. Double diffusion is out of scope; its mixing is absorbed by the entrainment closure.

WHAT THE CURRENT STACK ASSUMES (the five homogeneities stage 3 exists to remove — the design touches nothing else):
H1. The refractive index n is a per-scene constant → rays do not bend inside the volume.
H2. a and b are spatial constants → optical depth cannot be computed per ray.
H3. The halo kernel is spatially uniform → local turbidity differences do not become local blur differences.
H4. There is no path radiance — light scattered into the line of sight inside the volume (the veiling glow; visible light shafts in the water) is not computed.
H5. Single scattering only — defensible at today's scenes (path optical depth cL ≈ 0.2); an unmeasured assumption at cL → 1+.

THE DESIGN UNDER REVIEW (summary; the claims below are what you judge):
- v4-3.1 — GRIN transport core + thermal schlieren (freshwater pond). Introduce the index field n(x), with n(S,T,λ) from the Quan & Fry (1995) empirical fit (400–700 nm, 0–30 °C, S 0–35); integrate the ray equation d/ds(n·dr/ds) = ∇n with a Runge–Kutta scheme (standard schlieren / BOS practice). First field: bed-heated plumes — positions from bed albedo / spring inputs, spacing of order the depth, ΔT from the surface energy balance; there is no convection solver (by the ruling above), only its products are parametrised. Verification: (i) the homogeneous-n limit reproduces the current stack; (ii) the layered n(z) limit reproduces the Snell staircase; (iii) agreement with the analytic arc in a constant-∇n field; (iv) the derived displacement band for bed heating (apparent displacement 1–4 mm over a 0.5 m path) is checked as a derivation check, NOT as a target to hit.
- v4-3.2 — Inhomogeneous RTE. Fields a(x), b(x), CDOM(x); per-ray optical depth τ = ∫c ds along the bent paths from 3.1; the v16 FF-CDF halo kernel evaluated at local (τ, b/c); a path-radiance (veil) term added to the composite, separately exportable on its own for verification. Inputs: turbidity / CDOM fields (from field generators such as inflow plumes, or input maps) plus the bent paths of 3.1. Verification: (i) the homogeneous-field limit reproduces v16's numbers; (ii) statistical agreement with a delta-tracking (Woodcock) Monte Carlo reference on small grids (band energies of the deposit, the halo containment curve); (iii) a multiple-scattering decision measurement — MC with scattering order capped at 1 vs uncapped, on the same fields, sweeping cL = 0.2 / 0.5 / 1 / 2 — to decide H5 by numbers, not by preference.
- v4-3.3 — Two-layer density lane (brackish gate). Two-layer shallow-water dynamics (g′ coupling) + entrainment closure w_e = E(Ri)·ΔU (Ellison & Turner 1959 experimental form, Turner 1986 compilation, Hallberg analytic form); no resolved KH billows. The interface is a genuine sharp interface, treated as a step inside the GRIN transport (the layered limit of 3.1 applies directly). Inputs: recipe salinity as two-layer parameters (ΔS, layer thicknesses, inflow rate) + the stage-1 surface. Outputs: wedge position, front position and layer thicknesses over time; per-layer (a, b, n) to 3.1 / 3.2; the front's slick signature (roughness band-modulation input) to stage 1's film / strain lane — the foam / debris lines themselves remain stage 1's material-line lane. Verification: (i) front speed ~ √(g′h₁), and reproduction ranges for measured-scale convergence (≈0.05 s⁻¹) and downwelling (0.2–0.35 m/s); (ii) entrainment coefficients taken directly from Turner (1986) — not left unpinned; (iii) a freshwater analogue (warm / turbid inflow spreading over colder / clearer pond water) runs the same lane with no salt.
- v4-3.4 — Field time evolution + integrated verification. Decides how the fields live inside the 8-second loop (plume rise, front motion, statistically stationary renewal), and runs stage-3 scenes through the three verification sizes (main: 1.4755 m × 1.0828 m window at 0.6 m depth; sub: 4.8 m × 3.52 m at 4.0 m depth; contrast: 2.4 m × 1.76 m at 0.6 m depth). Loop closure is a numerical requirement and is permitted only in forms that do not bend the field physics (generation with periodic boundaries). Judgement: the existing gate set (energy conservation; the line-depth distribution diagnostic; the complementary pattern discriminator) plus the new stage-3 measurements (deflection-angle map, τ map, separated veil image).

CLAIMS — for EACH, give a verdict: STANDS / WEAKENED / WRONG, plus the strongest counterargument you have, plus a failure condition (an observation or measurement that would kill it):
C1. GRIN bending is first-order on the sun side (sun → bed) as well as on the view side (bed → eye): schlieren displace the caustics deposit itself, not only the bed's appearance. A design that includes only one side is physically incomplete.
C2. The apparent displacement from bed-heated plumes exceeds the pixel threshold at the main verification size (depth ≈ 0.6 m class) — the derivation gives 1–4 mm, i.e. several pixels. This magnitude is the reason bed heating is chosen as stage 3's first field, and recording cool-skin convection only (sub-pixel) is correct.
C3. A parametrised plume field (positions, spacing and ΔT determined from the surface energy balance and bed inputs) is admissible under the governing principle as a "labelled input" — the same status previously granted to secondary-flow surface patterns taken as input. The absence of a convection solver does not forbid using this field.
C4. Generalising to inhomogeneous RTE needs no new kernel shape: evaluating the v16 FF-CDF kernel at local (τ, b/c) suffices; what must change is the path-integral bookkeeping, not the kernel.
C5. The path-radiance (veil) term can be built now, against the current fixed top-down view, without waiting for stage 4 (camera); at stage 4, only the eye-side end needs re-pointing.
C6. The correct verification reference is a delta-tracking Monte Carlo tracer. Solvers of the HydroLight class, which assume plane-parallel geometry, cannot represent our geometry (finite patch + bed) and therefore cannot serve as the reference.
C7. Multiple scattering is adequately covered by single scattering at today's scenes (cL ≈ 0.2), but can become first-order in brackish / turbid scenes (cL → 1+). The boundary should not be drawn by taste; it should be decided by the controlled comparison against order-capped MC.
C8. A halocline should be treated as a sharp interface (a step) within the transport, not as continuous GRIN — whereas a temperature plume, if staircased, has its gradients crushed and becomes a different object. Splitting the representation between the two fields is not arbitrary; it is the difference in the interfaces' physical reality.
C9. The entrainment closure E(Ri) (the Ellison & Turner family of experimental curves) is flume / overflow-scale data, but it transfers to ≈0.6 m-class creek scenes without modification. It stands in the same position as Dolcetti's flume data in stage 1.
C10. Stage 3 needs no complete 3-D volume solver (a PDE evolving velocity and density fields in time). Holding the volume state as "time-varying 3-D fields (n, a, b) produced by reduced models" and doing only the transport exactly — that division of labour can still satisfy the standard of reproducing nature.
