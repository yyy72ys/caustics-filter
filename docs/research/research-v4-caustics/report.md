# Underwater Caustics — Physics for a Physically-Based Caustics Renderer (v4)

## Summary

The existing renderer — Phillips/FFT spectrum → deterministic Jacobian transport → flat textured bottom, composite = ambient + direct sun × caustic ratio, statistical Smith/Beckmann shadowing only, spectral Beer–Lambert depth attenuation, no bottom geometry, no cast shadows, no scattering volume — is architecturally sound for exactly one of the seven topics below: the **bare caustic field itself (§0)**, whose network topology, heavy-tailed statistics and flicker are *derived quantities* of the Jacobian map, not free parameters. Every environmental factor then enters as a modification of one of four places in that pipeline: (1) the surface state (incidence geometry does not change it; currents and rain do), (2) the per-facet transmission (Fresnel vs angle, foam/spray masks), (3) the propagation volume (bubbles and sediment add a real scattering coefficient that Beer–Lambert with water-only $c(\lambda)$ cannot represent, and which specifically *washes out contrast* rather than just dimming), and (4) the receiver (a heightfield bottom changes the Jacobian itself, adds true geometric shadowing, and — only over bright sand in very shallow clear water — a diffuse bounce pedestal worth modelling).

Headline quantitative anchors, all sourced in the sections below:

- Refracted sun direction is confined to a cone of half-angle $\theta_c=\arcsin(1/1.333)=48.6^\circ$ no matter how low the sun is; Fresnel power reflectance at a flat air–water interface is $\approx$2 % at normal incidence, $\approx$6 % at 60°, $\approx$21 % at 75°, $\to$100 % at grazing — so low sun does not mainly *tilt* caustics, it *starves* them.
- Geometric caustic intensity diverges as $|x-x_c|^{-1/2}$ at fold lines; the measured near-surface irradiance PDF is correspondingly heavy-tailed (skewness $>3$, excess kurtosis $>20$ above 1 m; peaks $>10\times$ mean at low wind, $13\times$ measured at 0.86 m), and **no standard analytic PDF (lognormal, Gumbel, Fréchet, Pareto…) fits the top decile** — the tail must come out of the transport, not out of a fitted distribution.
- Bubbles become optically significant at void fractions as low as $f>10^{-6}$; inside breaking-wave clouds $b_{\rm bub}>10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, a single measured cloud at $f=2.2\times10^{-5}$ gave $b_{\rm bub}=1\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, and bubble entrainment can more than double remote-sensing reflectance within minutes.
- Opposing currents shorten and steepen waves (following currents do the reverse) via conservation of wave action $N=E/\sigma$; complete blocking occurs when the opposing current reaches the intrinsic group velocity (deep-water rule of thumb $U=-c/4$ at blocking in the USACE formulation) — a renderer that only *advects* a texture misses the dominant effect, which is a change of the spectrum itself.
- For splashes/spray there is, honestly, **no published quantification of their direct contribution to bottom caustics relative to wave caustics**. The defensible model is indirect: droplets act before/at the surface (converting direct beam to diffuse, which is measured to kill caustic flashes — flashes at depth need $\gtrsim$30 % direct light), and rain/spray impacts act *through the surface spectrum* (ring waves of 1–2 cm wavelength). Any renderer term claiming a percentage for "spray caustics" would be invented.
- Object shadows and caustics are the *same* Jacobian field read two ways plus one visibility function: defocused regions ($\det J>1$) are the dark complement that pays for the bright folds (energy conservation), and a cast shadow is a modulation applied at the *surface-entry point*, smeared by the sun disc (0.53°, compressed to $\approx$0.40° in water) — an effect that is almost always *smaller* than the wave-slope smearing (Cox–Munk rms slope $\approx$10–17° at moderate wind), which is why underwater shadows flicker and fragment rather than merely softening.

## Findings

### 0. The caustic field itself — why a network, why heavy tails, why it flickers

**(i) Governing physics.**

*The ray map and its Jacobian.* Let the surface be $z=h(\mathbf{x},t)$, $\mathbf{x}=(x,y)$. A solar ray incident along unit direction $\mathbf{s}$ (in air) refracts at surface point $\mathbf{x}_s$ into direction $\mathbf{t}(\mathbf{x}_s)$ given by Snell's law in vector form, with $n_1=1$, $n_2=n\approx1.333$:

$$n_1\sin\theta_i=n_2\sin\theta_t,\qquad 
\mathbf{t}=\eta\,\mathbf{s}+\big(\eta\cos\theta_i-\cos\theta_t\big)\,\mathbf{N},\quad \eta=n_1/n_2,$$

$\mathbf{N}$ the local (tilted) surface normal. Propagating to a receiver plane at depth $D$ defines a smooth 2-D map $\Phi:\mathbf{x}_s\mapsto\mathbf{x}_b$ (surface point → bottom point). Conservation of flux through the map gives the bottom irradiance from the direct beam:

$$E_b(\mathbf{x}_b)=E_0\;T_F(\theta_i)\;e^{-c(\lambda)L}\;\big|\det J\big|^{-1},\qquad 
J=\frac{\partial \mathbf{x}_b}{\partial \mathbf{x}_s},$$

with $T_F$ the Fresnel power transmittance, $L$ the in-water path length, $c=a+b$ the beam attenuation coefficient. In the paraxial (small-slope, near-vertical sun) limit this reduces to the familiar Hessian form used by Jacobian renderers,

$$\det J \;\approx\; \det\!\Big(I + D\,(1-\tfrac1n)\,H\Big),\qquad H=\nabla\nabla h,$$

(up to sign convention for $H$). **A caustic is exactly the locus $\det J=0$**: neighbouring rays cross, the map folds over itself, and ray density — hence geometric intensity — diverges.

*Why a network of lines ending in points (catastrophe structure).* This is not an accident of water. Berry (1976, "Waves and Thom's theorem") and Berry & Upstill (1980, "Catastrophe optics", *Progress in Optics* 18:257–346) showed that for a smooth 2-D ray mapping the only *structurally stable* singularities — the ones that survive arbitrary small perturbations of the surface — are **fold lines** ($A_2$) **meeting at cusp points** ($A_3$). Higher catastrophes (swallowtail $A_4$, elliptic/hyperbolic umbilics $D_4^\pm$) occur only at isolated instants/locations in an evolving field. Consequences that a renderer gets for free if the transport is right, and can never get from a texture:

- The bright pattern is generically a *network*: fold curves form closed/connected loops, cusps are the pointed vertices where two folds meet and where intensity is locally highest. Dera & Stramski (1986) already cite Berry & Nye (1977) for the generic forms of the observed bottom patterns (their Fig. 1, pattern at 1.5 m).
- Crossing a fold changes the number of rays reaching a receiver point by $\pm2$: inside a cusp-bounded region **3 distinct surface points** illuminate one bottom point; outside, **1**. The Jacobian sign flips inside the folded region — a deterministic forward-splat renderer must sum $|\det J|^{-1}$ over *all* pre-images, and a backward (gather) renderer must find all roots, or it will under-light precisely the brightest cells.
- Focal depth: for a sinusoidal component $a\cos kx$, curvature $\sim ak^2$ and the paraxial focal distance is $f\approx[n/(n-1)]\,(ak^2)^{-1}\approx4/(ak^2)$. Short, steep waves focus within centimetres–metres of the surface; long, flat waves focus at tens of metres. This is Schenck's (1957) classical conclusion, restated by Stramski & Dera: only short steep waves matter for near-surface flashing; it is why the caustic *scale* on a bottom at depth $D$ is selected by the surface spectrum — components with $f\approx D$ dominate the pattern at that depth, finer components have already crossed and blurred into a web, coarser ones have not yet focused.

*Singularity, and what makes it finite.* Near a fold, geometric intensity diverges one-sidedly as

$$E(x)\propto |x-x_c|^{-1/2}\quad (x\to x_c\ \text{from the lit side}),\qquad E=0\ \text{(geometric) on the dark side}.$$

Wave optics replaces this with the diffraction catastrophe: the fold is dressed by an **Airy pattern**, $E\propto \mathrm{Ai}^2(\zeta)$ — finite, first maximum at $\zeta=-1.0188$ *inside* the lit side, decaying oscillations behind it, exponential leakage into the geometric shadow; the cusp is dressed by the **Pearcey pattern**. Fringe scale near a fold varies as $(\lambda/a)^{2/3}$ ($a$ = aperture/curvature scale) — the $2/3$ power is the fold's Berry index signature. Peak wave-optical intensity scales as $k^{1/3}$ for a fold and $k^{1/2}$ for a cusp ($k=2\pi/\lambda$), i.e. cusps brighten faster as $\lambda\to0$ and dominate the extreme tail. **For a renderer, however, diffraction is almost never the operative regularizer**: at $\lambda\approx500$ nm the Airy width is micrometre-scale. The finite peak in any real or rendered image is set, in descending order of importance, by (a) the **finite sun disc** (0.53° in air, §1/§6), (b) the **receiver footprint / pixel / splat kernel**, (c) **volume scattering** along the path (§4), (d) surface micro-structure below the spectrum's cut-off, and only then (e) diffraction. A Jacobian renderer must therefore *impose* a regularization (kernel width tied to sun-disc angular size × path length, §6) — leaving $\det J\to0$ unclamped produces resolution-dependent, non-convergent brightness, which is a modelling error, not physical detail.

*Intensity statistics — why the tail is heavy, quantitatively.* Because $E\propto|\det J|^{-1}$ and $\det J$ passes smoothly through zero, the area of bottom with intensity above a threshold $E_*$ scales as $E_*^{-2}$ near folds (from the $|x|^{-1/2}$ law), i.e. a power-law-like tail $p(E)\sim E^{-3}$ asymptotically for pure fold geometry before regularization cuts it off — bright events are rare but not exponentially rare. Berry (1977) / Hannay (1983, *J. Phys. A* 16:L61) showed the moments $\langle I^m\rangle$ in the geometric limit are governed by the catastrophe hierarchy alone ("twinkling exponents" — universal rational powers of $k$), with the wave spectrum entering only the prefactor. Measurements match this picture and defeat simple fits:

- Gernez, Stramski & Darecki (2011, *JGR* 116:C00H07), 86 profiles, top 10 m, 532 nm, sunny: near-surface $E_d$ PDFs are **highly skewed and heavy-tailed; skewness $>3$ and excess kurtosis $>20$ at depths $<1$ m**; **no tested model — lognormal, Gumbel, Fréchet, log-logistic, Pareto — consistently fits the tail above the 90th percentile**; lognormal is adequate (within $\approx$20 %) *below* the 90th percentile; with depth the PDF relaxes to near-symmetric/normal, skewness and kurtosis $\to\approx0$ by $\approx$10 m.
- Flash climatology (Dera & Stramski 1986, *Oceanologia* 23:15–42; summarised in Veal et al. 2010): a "flash" is $E>1.5\bar E$ (Stramski's definition); frequencies up to $\approx$200 min$^{-1}$ at 1 m (over 350 min$^{-1}$ reported under ideal conditions); frequency falls **exponentially** with flash intensity, $E>5\bar E$ occurring at most $\sim$1 min$^{-1}$; Schenck's theoretical maximum $\approx6\times$ mean, while later measurements/simulations exceed it near the surface: Gernez et al. measured peaks $13\times$ mean at 0.86 m/532 nm, Hieronymi & Macke's Monte Carlo gives $>10\times$ in the top metres at wind $\le5$ m s$^{-1}$, up to $11\times$ with ultra-gravity waves, $>7\times$ down to 3 m, 50 % enhancements ($1.5\times$) still at 30–35 m, and $\pm$10 % variability at 100 m in clear water.
- Focusing is *maximal at light winds 2–5 m s$^{-1}$*, clear sky, high sun, clear water (Dera & Stramski 1986) — stronger wind steepens small waves so they focus *above* a given receiver and add foam/bubbles (§3–4); the naive "more wind = stronger caustics everywhere" is wrong at fixed depth.

*Time statistics (flicker).* Two distinct timescales, often conflated: (1) the **envelope** set by the surface-wave field — Hieronymi & Macke report the mean fluctuation period is approximately **half the dominant surface-wave period** in the diffuse/mixed case (Gernez & Antoine 2009), and point fluctuations span fractions of a second to $\sim$20 s; (2) the **flash width** at a fixed point, set by a caustic line sweeping past: most probable duration **10–30 ms at 1 m** (at the $0.5E$ level; overall range 10–300 ms), duration PDF skewed and approximately lognormal, durations *increasing* with depth as intensity falls (Dera & Stramski 1986). In spectral terms the flicker at a point is the surface spectrum Doppler-mapped through the (nonlinear) Jacobian: temporal frequency $\approx$ (caustic pattern velocity)$^{-1}$ × (pattern spatial frequency), pattern velocity being the phase/group velocity of the focusing component plus any current (§2). There is no universal flicker spectrum independent of the wave spectrum — it is a derived diagnostic.

**(ii) Independent vs derived.** Everything in this section is **derived** from (surface spectrum, sun direction, depth, $n$, $c(\lambda)$). Network topology, ray multiplicity, tail shape, focal selection by depth, and flicker spectrum must *emerge*; if a v4 feature list contains knobs for "caustic sharpness", "network scale" or "flicker rate" independent of the spectrum and depth, those knobs are unphysical. The genuinely independent inputs are only: spectrum parameters, $D$, sun, and the regularization width (which should itself be derived from sun-disc size and path length, §6).

**(iii) Implementation route.** Keep deterministic Jacobian transport — it is the correct geometric limit. Required changes: (a) **multi-pre-image handling** — forward splatting handles this naturally (every surface sample splats once; folded regions receive 3 splats); a backward gather must sum all roots or it will miss the folded contribution. Do not take $|\det J|^{-1}$ from a single backward ray. (b) **Explicit regularization**: convolve/clamp with a kernel whose width is $w \approx L\tan(\Delta\theta_{w,\odot})$ (in-water sun-disc size, §6) plus pixel footprint; equivalently clamp $E/E_0$ at $\sim A_{\rm cell}/A_{\rm kernel}$. Document the clamp as the sun-disc term, not as an artistic "max intensity". (c) **Validation targets** (cheap, decisive): energy conservation $\int E_b\,dA = T_F E_0 A\, e^{-c\bar L}$ to within discretisation error; PDF skewness/kurtosis vs depth trending like Gernez et al. (skew $>3$, kurt $>20$ at $<1$ m in the idealised clear case, relaxing by $\sim$10 m); flash rate/duration at 1 m in the 10–30 ms / $\mathcal O(10^2\,\mathrm{min^{-1}})$ range for a light-wind spectrum. If the renderer's PDF is Gaussian/lognormal by construction, it has already failed the tail test.

**(iv) Honest limits.** The asymptotic tail exponent and twinkling exponents are derivable; the *prefactors* (absolute flash rates at a given sea state) depend on the high-$k$ tail of the real spectrum (capillary range), which Phillips' spectrum does not model correctly (it is a gravity-wave, fully-developed-sea form; Tessendorf's implementation suppresses small waves ad hoc). Expect to calibrate the spectrum's high-$k$ cut-off / capillary extension (e.g. Elfouhaily et al.'s unified spectrum, used in the ocean-optics Monte Carlo work) against measured CV/skewness, not against pictures. Diffraction (Airy/Pearcey dressing) is real but visually irrelevant at solar broadband and metre scales — do not spend machinery on it; spend it on the sun-disc kernel.

### 1. Incidence angle — sun elevation

**(i) Governing physics.**

*Snell compression / Snell window.* For solar zenith angle in air $\theta_s$ (from the vertical), the refracted zenith angle in water is

$$\theta_w=\arcsin\!\big(\sin\theta_s/n\big),\qquad n=1.333,$$

e.g. $\theta_s=0^\circ\!\to\!0^\circ$, $30^\circ\!\to\!22.0^\circ$, $45^\circ\!\to\!32.0^\circ$, $60^\circ\!\to\!40.5^\circ$, $75^\circ\!\to\!46.4^\circ$, $90^\circ\!\to\!48.6^\circ$. **All** direct sunlight, from any sky direction, is compressed into the Snell cone of half-angle $\theta_c=\arcsin(1/n)=48.6^\circ$ (full window $97.2$–$97.5^\circ$; values 48.6° and 48.75° both appear in the literature for $n=1.333$–1.34). Corollary: the direct beam in water can never be more than 48.6° from vertical, so bottom displacement of the whole caustic field is bounded: $\Delta = D\tan\theta_w \le 1.13\,D$, and the pattern is sheared, not arbitrarily raked.

*Fresnel transmission vs angle.* Unpolarised power reflectance at a flat interface,

$$R=\tfrac12(R_s+R_p),\quad
R_s=\Big(\frac{n_1\cos\theta_i-n_2\cos\theta_t}{n_1\cos\theta_i+n_2\cos\theta_t}\Big)^{\!2},\quad
R_p=\Big(\frac{n_2\cos\theta_i-n_1\cos\theta_t}{n_2\cos\theta_i+n_1\cos\theta_t}\Big)^{\!2},\quad T_F=1-R,$$

gives $R_0=((n-1)/(n+1))^2=0.020$ at normal incidence, and (standard tabulated values) $R\approx$ 2.0 % (0°), 2.1 % (30°), 2.8 % (45°), **6.0 % (60°)**, **21 % (75°)**, $\to$100 % at grazing. Brewster angle $\arctan n=53.1^\circ$ ($R_p=0$; relevant only if polarisation is ever modelled). On a *wavy* surface the effective (Preisendorfer–Mobley) reflectance is an average over the Cox–Munk slope distribution, $\sigma^2=0.003+5.12\times10^{-3}U_{10}$ (total mean-square slope; upwind/crosswind components $\approx0.00316U$ / $0.00192U$ plus intercepts), which smooths the flat-surface curve — glints transmit/reflect at local angles far from $\theta_s$ — but preserves the conclusion: **the dominant low-sun effect is loss of transmitted flux, not geometry.** Guardado & Sánchez-Crespo's widely used real-time method simply assumes noon/equator sun; Yuksel & Keyser note such single-geometry methods are "highly inaccurate" away from overhead sun — v4 must not inherit that assumption.

*Stretching and anisotropy.* With oblique $\mathbf{t}$, the map $\Phi$ acquires a systematic shear: a surface patch's footprint on a horizontal bottom is displaced by $D\tan\theta_w$ along the solar azimuth and stretched along that azimuth by a factor that grows like $1/\cos$-type projection terms (footprint elongation $\sim \sec\theta_w$ for the mean ray, compounded by the differential term $\partial \tan\theta_w/\partial\theta$ across wave slopes). Two competing consequences: (a) caustic *lines* are elongated/anisotropic — pattern correlation length along the sun azimuth exceeds that across it, increasingly so as $\theta_s$ grows (at $\theta_s=60^\circ$, $\theta_w=40.5^\circ$, path length is already $D/\cos\theta_w=1.32D$); (b) the *focusing geometry rotates*: the effective curvature that matters is the surface Hessian projected onto the plane containing $\mathbf{t}$, so focusing by waves travelling along the sun azimuth differs from focusing by cross-waves — the pattern becomes directionally selective in a way a vertical-sun Jacobian (which sees the full Hessian symmetrically) cannot reproduce. Path length also grows, $L=D/\cos\theta_{\rm local}$, strengthening spectral attenuation and scattering washout at low sun even before Fresnel losses.

*Intensity statistics vs elevation.* Mean bottom irradiance falls steeply (Fresnel + path length + $\cos$ projection on the receiver). The *normalised* fluctuation statistics are, counter-intuitively, reported to be comparatively insensitive to sun altitude: Hieronymi & Macke, summarising Gernez & Antoine (2009), state the coefficient of variation CV "is nearly independent of the sun altitude or the water clarity" while the mean fluctuation *period* tracks the waves. By contrast the **diffuse fraction** is decisive: wave focusing is a direct-beam phenomenon; Hieronymi & Macke's Monte Carlo shows light flashes ($1.5\times$) at 30 m under clear sky but **no flashes develop under 100 % diffuse light — at least $\sim$30 % direct sunlight is required** — and at 7 m depth $E_d$ ranged 29–193 % of mean (clear) vs only 62–72 % (overcast). Low sun under a clear sky keeps a direct beam (so caustics persist, dimmer and sheared); low sun under haze/cloud loses them by diffuseness, a different mechanism than elevation per se.

**(ii) Independent vs derived.** Solar zenith/azimuth are **independent inputs** (scene parameters). Everything else — $\theta_w$, footprint shear, anisotropy, $T_F$, path length — is **derived**, facet by facet. Do not add a global "low-sun caustic strength" knob: the dimming is $T_F(\theta_i^{\rm local})$ integrated over the slope distribution, which the transport already computes if Fresnel is evaluated per facet rather than once for the flat surface.

**(iii) Implementation route.** (1) Generalise the Jacobian transport to arbitrary $\mathbf{s}$: refract per facet with the vector Snell form, propagate along $\mathbf{t}$ (not vertically), intersect the receiver plane/heightfield (§5). This is a change of ray direction and of the Jacobian's linearisation point, not new machinery. (2) Evaluate $T_F$ **per surface sample** at its local incidence angle (including polarisation-averaged form above) instead of a single flat-surface factor; keep the existing statistical Smith/Beckmann term only for what it is — microfacet self-shadowing of the *surface* — and not as a substitute. (3) Use path length $L$ per ray in the Beer–Lambert factor (already spectral). (4) Sun-disc kernel must be re-centred on $\mathbf{t}$ and compressed: in-water disc angular radius $\approx (\cos\theta_s/(n\cos\theta_w))\times0.265^\circ$ ($\approx0.20^\circ$ half-angle at noon, i.e. $\approx$0.40° full width). No new passes required.

**(iv) Honest limits.** Per-facet Fresnel + Snell is exact geometric optics. What is *not* derivable from the spectrum alone is the effective surface reflectance at high wind/low sun, because foam coverage (§3) and the capillary slope tail (Cox–Munk is a glitter statistic, not a spectrum) control the average; calibrate the integrated $T_F$ against Preisendorfer–Mobley/Hydrolight-type values if absolute radiometry matters. Polarisation (Brewster, skylight polarisation) is a real second-order effect on $T_F$ that unpolarised Fresnel averages away — acceptable to omit, but state it.

### 2. Currents and flow

**(i) Governing physics.**

*(a) Effect on the wave field — this is the main effect.* In a current $\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{x})$ (surface/depth-averaged as appropriate), the observed (absolute) frequency of a component $\mathbf{k}$ is Doppler-shifted from its intrinsic frequency $\sigma$:

$$\Omega(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{k})=\sigma+\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{U},\qquad 
\sigma^2=gk\tanh(kd)\ \ \text{(intrinsic dispersion)},$$

the form used by Gallet & Young (2014, *J. Mar. Res.* 72:105–126) for swell refraction by currents, $\Omega=\sqrt{gk}+\mathbf{u}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{k}$ in deep water. Rays of wave action obey Hamilton's equations with Hamiltonian $\Omega$: $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\partial\Omega/\partial\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{c}_g+\mathbf{U}$, $\dot{\mathbf{k}}=-\partial\Omega/\partial\mathbf{x}=-(\nabla\mathbf{U})^{T}\mathbf{k}-\partial\sigma/\partial d\,\nabla d$. Hence spatially varying $\mathbf{U}$ **refracts** waves (direction changes, ray convergence/divergence — Gallet & Young show mesoscale vorticity deflects swell into multipath bundles, measurably shifting arrival direction), and a current *gradient along* the propagation direction changes $|\mathbf{k}|$: conservation of wave action (Bretherton & Garrett), with action density $N=E/\sigma$ and flux $(\mathbf{c}_g+\mathbf{U})N$ conserved in steady state (the basis of SWAN-type spectral models, action balance $\partial_tN+\nabla\!\cdot\!((\mathbf{c}_g+\mathbf{U})N)+\partial_\theta(c_\theta N)+\partial_\sigma(c_\sigma N)=S$):

- **Opposing current strengthening** ($\partial U/\partial x<0$ along propagation): $\sigma$ fixed by $\Omega$ conservation forces $k$ **up** → wavelength **shortens**, and action conservation forces $E$ up → waves **steepen** (height up, length down). Steepness can reach breaking (Miche limit $H_{\max}/L=0.142$ deep water).
- **Following current**: $k$ down, wavelength stretches, height and steepness fall.
- **Blocking**: when $\mathbf{U}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}}=-c_g$ no action propagates upstream; deep-water USACE (CETN IV-9/IV-17, after Jonsson) blocking at $U=-c/4$ phase-speed terms — waves steepen and break before the limit. In renderer terms: there are (sun, spectrum, current) combinations for which the assumed homogeneous spectrum *cannot exist* at a location — the surface state itself is current-dependent.

Magnitude check: a 2 m s$^{-1}$ ebb against a shallow-water wave of phase speed 7.7 m s$^{-1}$ cuts ground speed $\approx$26 %, compressing wavelength by the same factor and concentrating energy accordingly — order-unity changes to caustic scale at tidal inlets/river mouths, negligible ($\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{U}/\sigma\ll1$) for the short capillary-gravity components that dominate near-surface focusing in the open ocean at typical 0.1–0.5 m s$^{-1}$ currents. The effect is strongly $k$-selective: $\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{U}$ grows with $k$, but $\sigma\sim\sqrt{gk}$ also grows; the ratio $Uk/\sigma=U\sqrt{k/g}$ (deep water) means a 1 m s$^{-1}$ current shifts a 1 m wave ($\sigma\approx7.9$ rad s$^{-1}$... $k=6.3$) by $\mathcal O(1)$ but a 10 cm wave ($k=63$, $\sigma\approx25$) by $\approx$2.5× its intrinsic frequency fraction $Uk/\sigma\approx2.5$ — in practice short waves are locally wind-generated and equilibrate with the *local* wind-over-current, which is why operational models advect action rather than applying a uniform Doppler shift.

*(b) Effect on the optics, given the wave field.* (1) **Advection**: a *uniform* $\mathbf{U}$ adds $\mathbf{U}t$ to the surface pattern's phase, $\omega\to\omega-\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{U}$ in the lab frame; the caustic pattern on the bottom translates/deforms at the focusing components' group velocity plus $\mathbf{U}$. This is the only effect a "scroll the texture" model captures, and it is the smallest of the three. (2) **Turbulence / refractive-index microstructure**: temperature/salinity microstructure gives $\delta n/n\sim10^{-7}$–$10^{-5}$-scale fluctuations; oceanic scintillation from this is a studied effect for lasercom (lognormal at scintillation index $\sigma_I^2<0.1$) but is orders of magnitude below surface-wave lensing for broadband sunlight imaging — not defensible to model for caustics. (3) **Suspended-sediment plumes**: currents resuspend/advect sediment; optically this is a spatially varying $b(\mathbf{x},\lambda)$ and $K_d$. Suspended particulate matter is typically the dominant attenuator in coastal water: NIWA's stocktake gives $K_d(\mathrm{PAR})=\ln(1/T)/\Delta z$, euphotic depth $z_{eu}=4.6/K_d$, beam $c=\ln(1/T)/r$ with visual range $\approx4.8/c(550)$; Changjiang-estuary measurements show suspended sediment is the leading control on $K_d(\mathrm{PAR})$, ahead of CDOM/salinity and chlorophyll; Australian-coastal satellite analysis finds coastal $K_d(490)\approx0.3\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ tracking total suspended solids. A plume therefore does to caustics what any scattering does (§4): adds path radiance (pedestal) and attenuates the direct beam $\propto e^{-cL}$, reducing *contrast* $ (E_{\max}-\bar E)/\bar E$ roughly by the direct/total ratio, not merely dimming.

**(ii) Independent vs derived.** $\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{x},t)$ is an **independent input field** (like wind). The modified spectrum is **derived** — but *not* by the renderer's current architecture, which assumes a homogeneous FFT tile: a spatially varying current breaks tile periodicity and the $\Omega=\sigma+\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{U}$ shift is $\mathbf{x}$-dependent. Sediment concentration is independent (or derived from $\mathbf{U}$ via a resuspension model — out of scope); its optical coefficients are derived from concentration only through empirical specific-scattering coefficients (§(iv)).

**(iii) Implementation route — what is defensible.** Tier 1 (do this): **uniform-current Doppler/advection** — replace $\omega(\mathbf{k})=\sqrt{gk\tanh kd}$ in the spectrum animation by $\Omega=\omega+\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{U}$; costs nothing, exact for uniform $\mathbf{U}$, and correctly changes pattern motion and component phase speeds. Tier 2 (do if currents are a headline feature): **piecewise-homogeneous action adjustment** — for a slowly varying 1-D current $U(x)$, remap each component's $k$ and amplitude by action conservation, $\sigma=\Omega-\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{U}=$ const along rays, $E\propto\sigma\,[(\mathbf{c}_g+\mathbf{U})]^{-1}$-type scaling (SWAN/Jonsson formulae), applied as a spatially varying spectrum parameter set over a few zones rather than a single tile; add a breaking cap ($H/L\le0.142$) where opposing gradients steepen. Tier 3 (plumes): a **spatially varying $c(\lambda,\mathbf{x})$ / $K_d$ field** sampled along each transport ray (line integral instead of $cL$) plus a matching diffuse pedestal term (§4) — new machinery: a low-resolution 3-D/2.5-D turbidity texture; no new transport pass. **Not defensible**: full action-balance PDE solution inside a renderer, wave–current nonlinear (NLS/modulational-instability, rogue-wave) corrections, or turbulence scintillation — all are research codes, and their visual signature at caustic scales is second-order vs Tier 1–2.

**(iv) Honest limits.** Action conservation is linear theory; near blocking and in strong gradients ($\nabla U$ comparable to $\sigma$) waves break and dissipate — the *amount* of dissipation is empirical (SWAN source terms are calibrated). Sediment optics: $b$ per unit TSS (specific scattering, $\sim$0.3–1 m² g$^{-1}$ range in coastal literature, mineralogy-dependent) must be calibrated per site/water type; there is no first-principles $b(\mathrm{TSS})$. Uniform-current Doppler is exact; everything beyond it is an approximation whose error grows with $|\nabla\mathbf{U}|\,L_{\rm tile}/\bar U$.

### 3. Splashes and spray — droplets at and above the surface

**(i) Governing physics.** Three physically distinct populations, with different optics:

1. **Airborne spray/rain droplets above the surface** (sea spray from bursting bubbles and wind tearing, spume at high wind; raindrops). Radii: spray aerosol $\sim$1–100 µm, spume/rain $\sim$0.1–3 mm — i.e. size parameter $x=2\pi r/\lambda\gg1$ (geometric/Mie-large-particle regime, extinction efficiency $Q_{\rm ext}\to2$). Their effect on the *downward* beam is as a thin atmospheric scattering layer: it removes flux from the direct beam and re-distributes it into a diffuse halo. By §1's result (flashes need $\gtrsim$30 % direct light; 100 % diffuse → no flashes at depth, $E_d$ range at 7 m collapses from 29–193 % to 62–72 % of mean), **the mechanism by which a droplet layer hurts caustics is direct→diffuse conversion**, exactly like cloud. A droplet layer does not create its own bottom lensing pattern of any consequence: each droplet is a ball lens with focal length $\sim r\,n/(2(n-1))\approx2r$ *in air*, i.e. millimetres below itself, kilometres of propagation short of the bottom — droplet foci dissipate in the surface layer.
2. **Impact structures at the surface (rain, spray fallback, splashing)**: a drop impact produces a crater/cavity, crown, central stalk/jet, secondary droplets, and an expanding **ring wave** — gravity-capillary waves of **wavelength 1–2 cm**, visible over a patch **25–50 cm in diameter** (Wetzel 1990; Bliven et al. 1993, *Int. J. Remote Sens.* 14:2315 — ring waves dominate the radar backscatter signature of rain-agitated water; tank rain rates 3–30 mm h$^{-1}$ give small-scale elevation variance comparable to light–moderate wind). Simultaneously rain **damps** mechanically generated gravity waves of wavelength $\gtrsim$ a few cm (rain-induced turbulence; Reynolds 1900's "knocking down the sea"; Laxague et al. 2020, *GRL* — rain also forms a fresh surface lens and modifies near-surface currents). Net spectral action: energy removed from $>$few-cm gravity waves, energy added at 1–2 cm capillary-gravity scales.
3. **Foam/whitecaps (the surface residue of breaking and splashing)**: foam is a high-albedo, diffusely reflecting, *non-transmitting* patch of surface. Koepke (1984, *Appl. Opt.*): **effective reflectance of whitecaps $\approx$22 % in the visible** (fresh dense foam alone 0.4–0.6 albedo vs $\approx$0.05 for open ocean — Evans & Stride 2010); Frouin et al. (1996, *JGR* 101:14361): foam reflectance falls $\approx$40 % by 0.85 µm, 50 % by 1.02 µm, 85 % by 1.65 µm (bubbles in the underlayer enhance absorption in the NIR); coverage $\approx$4 % at wind $\approx$14 m s$^{-1}$ (Monahan-type $W\propto U^{3.4}$ laws). Where foam sits, direct transmission $\approx0$: foam is a *hole* in the caustic-generating surface and a bright diffuse reflector above it.

*Relative contribution — the honest headline.* **No paper in the ocean-optics or rendering literature quantifies a direct contribution of spray/splash droplets to bottom caustic patterns, relative to surface-wave caustics.** The quantified effects are all (a) atmospheric-side (aerosol optical depth, radar signatures), (b) spectral at the surface (ring waves), or (c) foam coverage/reflectance. Given focal-length scaling (droplet foci die within mm) and the direct-fraction argument, the physically defensible statement is: droplets' caustic-relevant effect is $\mathcal O$(their direct-beam extinction + their modification of the surface spectrum + foam masking), and any *additional* sparkle term would be an artistic invention. ("Sparkle" seen by a camera near the surface is sun glint off droplet/foam facets — a *surface* rendering effect, not a bottom-illumination effect.)

**(ii) Independent vs derived.** Rain rate / spray production are **independent** environmental inputs (functions of wind and breaking in reality — foam coverage is derivable from $U_{10}$ via Monahan/Koepke laws to within their scatter). Ring-wave spectral energy is **derived in form** (1–2 cm band) but its amplitude vs rain rate is empirical. Foam mask placement is derived from the wave field in principle (breaking crests, Jacobian-fold/foam criteria as in Tessendorf-style rendering) but in practice calibrated.

**(iii) Implementation route.** (1) **Direct-fraction parameter**: split the solar input into direct + diffuse fractions $f_{\rm dir}+f_{\rm diff}=1$ (already needed for cloud, §1); spray/rain extinction simply moves flux from $f_{\rm dir}$ to $f_{\rm diff}$ (diffuse part contributes a smooth, caustic-free bottom illumination $\propto e^{-K_dD}$). Zero new transport machinery — the caustic pass runs on the direct part only. This is the single most defensible "spray" feature. (2) **Spectrum modification for rain**: add a narrow spectral bump at $k=2\pi/(0.01\text{–}0.02\,\mathrm{m})$ and apply a damping factor to gravity components $\lambda\gtrsim$ few cm, both scaled by rain rate; the existing Jacobian transport then produces whatever fine, shallow-focusing caustic contribution ring waves actually make (their focal depth $f\approx4/(ak^2)$ is centimetres, so at metre depths they contribute a fine-grained *blur/roughness* to the pattern rather than distinct lines — which the transport will show automatically). (3) **Foam mask**: a per-surface-sample coverage probability $W(U)$ (or a crest/Jacobian-based deterministic mask advected with the surface), setting $T_F=0$ and surface albedo to foam values (0.22 effective / spectral Frouin curve) on masked samples. New machinery: only the mask field and the $f_{\rm dir}$ split.

**(iv) Honest limits.** The ring-wave amplitude vs rain-rate relation comes from radar/tank studies, not from optical caustic measurements; treat its gain as a calibration knob with a literature-shaped prior (variance comparable to light–moderate wind at 3–30 mm h$^{-1}$). Spume production at $U>15$ m s$^{-1}$ and its optical depth are poorly constrained even in climate research. And the negative result above is a real limit: if a stakeholder asks "how much of this bottom pattern is spray?", the literature-supported answer is "no measurable direct share has ever been reported; the effect is through direct-beam dilution, ring waves, and foam".

### 4. Bubbles / aeration — bubble clouds in the volume

**(i) Governing physics.**

*Microphysics.* Bubbles in water have relative refractive index $m=1/1.333=0.75$ — a strong *negative* lens / efficient scatterer, with sizes typically large vs $\lambda$, so Mie theory with $Q_{\rm sca}\to2$ applies. For a size distribution $n(a)$ (bubbles per volume per radius), void fraction and scattering coefficient are

$$f=\int\tfrac43\pi a^3 n(a)\,da,\qquad 
b_{\rm bub}=\int Q_{\rm sca}\pi a^2 n(a)\,da\;\approx\;\frac{2f}{r_{\rm eff}},\qquad 
r_{\rm eff}=\frac{\int a^3n\,da}{\int a^2n\,da},$$

the compact form $b=2f/r_{\rm eff}$ used in the bubble-climatology literature (Flatau/Koike et al., *QJRMS* 2000, arXiv:physics/0006060). Absorption by clean bubbles is negligible — bubbles are (to first order) **pure scatterers**; organic coatings mainly enhance *backscattering* (up to $\times4$ for coated vs clean, Zhang et al. 1998, cited in Terrill et al.). Size distributions are two-regime power laws split at the Hinze scale $a_H\approx0.5$–1.5 mm radius (Deane & Stokes 2002, *Nature* 418:839): $n(a)\propto a^{-3/2}$ for $a<a_H$ (jet/drop-impact production) and $n(a)\propto a^{-10/3}$ for $a>a_H$ (turbulent fragmentation); acoustically measured oceanic populations span radii $\mathcal O(10$–$1000)$ µm.

*Numbers (ocean).* Terrill, Melville & Stramski (2001, *JGR* 106:16815–16823), field data at $U_{10}=15$ m s$^{-1}$, $H_{1/3}=3.2$ m: bubble contribution to scattering is **highly variable — spanning $\sim$5 decades over $\sim$10 minutes**; at 0.7 m depth, optical effects on backscattering (hence reflectance) become **significant at void fractions above $10^{-6}$**, and inside bubble clouds $b_{\rm bub}$ **exceeds $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$** (compare clear-ocean $b\sim$few$\times10^{-3}$–$10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$). Stramski (2024, synthesising Stramski & Tegowski 2001): a cloud with maximum $f=2.2\times10^{-5}$ at 1.8 m produced $b_{\rm bub}=1\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ — i.e. a 1 m path through such a cloud transmits $e^{-1}=37$ % unscattered — while a few minutes later concentrations were 3–4 orders lower. Stramski & Tegowski (2001, *JGR* 106:31345): an entrainment event at 10 m s$^{-1}$ wind **more than doubled** remote-sensing reflectance, on timescales of minutes, with a spectral (green/yellow) shift. Background climatology: $f\propto U^{4.4}$-type laws, $f\approx10^{-8}$ at $U=6.2$ m s$^{-1}$ and $10^{-7}$ at 10.5 m s$^{-1}$ (at 2 m), $f$ falling from $\sim10^{-6}$ at 0.3 m to $\sim10^{-7}$ at 2.7 m; individual clouds persist for minutes and reach mean depths $\approx4H_s$ (some $6H_s$). Piskozub et al. (2009) 3-D Monte Carlo: for realistic cloud sizes a 1-D (plane-parallel) treatment is *inadequate* — clouds are localised objects with local brightening above/inside and shadowing below.

*Effect on caustics specifically.* No scattering volume "blurs" in the image-processing sense; it (a) attenuates the direct (unscattered) beam as $e^{-(c_w+c_{\rm bub})L}$, (b) converts removed flux into a broadly distributed scattered field that arrives at the bottom as a **pedestal** (brightening/washout — upwelling irradiance and radiance are the most strongly affected quantities in Stramski & Tegowski's calculations), and (c) therefore reduces caustic *contrast* approximately by the direct fraction of total bottom illumination, $\mathcal C\approx E_{\rm dir}/(E_{\rm dir}+E_{\rm scat})$, while raising the mean in the cloud's vicinity (multiple scattering returns flux that a pure-absorption model would lose). Dense aeration near injection points (waterfalls, propellers, breaking crests in tanks/pools) reaches void fractions $10^{-2}$–$10^{-1}$+ (whitecap-plume measurements report 0.3–0.4 inside active plumes) — there $b_{\rm bub}=2f/r_{\rm eff}$ with $r_{\rm eff}\sim$100 µm gives $b\sim$200–2000 m$^{-1}$: optically thick within centimetres, i.e. a white, caustic-destroying curtain, qualitatively different from the oceanic dilute regime. A renderer spanning "agitated water" must handle both regimes with the same $b(f,r_{\rm eff})$ law.

**(ii) Independent vs derived.** Void fraction field $f(\mathbf{x},t)$ (or equivalently cloud geometry + density) is an **independent input** (in nature derived from breaking, which a spectrum renderer does not simulate). $b_{\rm bub}$, phase function, and contrast loss are **derived** from $(f, n(a))$ via Mie/$r_{\rm eff}$ — with the practical reduction that, for $Q_{\rm sca}\to2$ bubbles, $b$ depends on the distribution *only* through $r_{\rm eff}$: a one-parameter family. Backscattering fraction is *not* so reducible (coatings, distribution shape).

**(iii) Implementation route.** Replace the scalar Beer–Lambert factor with a **two-component volume**: direct term $e^{-\int(c_w(\lambda)+b_{\rm bub})\,ds}$ along each transport ray (line integral through a bubble-density texture — same machinery as the sediment plume in §2, Tier 3; share it), plus a **single-scattering / pedestal term**: scattered flux $\approx E_0\,(1-e^{-b_{\rm bub}L})$-weighted, distributed smoothly (in practice: add a blurred, depth-attenuated copy of the *mean* illumination, not of the caustic pattern — scattering at these phase functions (strongly forward-peaked for large bubbles, but with a broad halo after metre paths) destroys pattern detail above spatial frequencies $\sim1/L$). New machinery: (1) density field (texture or analytic cloud ellipsoids advected/rising with Stokes rise speed $v=\tfrac{2}{9}g a^2/\nu$-scaling for the small-bubble end), (2) line-integral attenuation in the transport pass, (3) pedestal accumulation pass. A full volume photon map (§5) is the high-end alternative and handles (2)+(3) together, at photon-map cost. Do **not** model bubbles as an increased blur radius on the caustic texture: blur conserves the wrong thing — scattering removes direct flux *and adds* flux elsewhere, changing both mean and contrast; a blur-only model gets the mean wrong in exactly the bright-cloud cases users will test.

**(iv) Honest limits.** $n(a)$ below $\sim$10 µm and the coated/clean mix are poorly known in situ; $b_{\rm bub}$ from $f$ alone carries the $r_{\rm eff}$ uncertainty (factor $\sim$2–3). The phase function of a polydisperse bubble population (needed for *where* the pedestal lands, and for any camera-in-water view) requires Mie integration over an assumed distribution — calibrate against Terrill/Stramski $b_b/b$ ratios. Temporal intermittency (5 decades in minutes) means any steady-$f$ render is a time-average that *understates* peak washout; if the product shows aeration events, $f(t)$ must be an animated input, not a constant. Pool/waterfall void fractions ($10^{-2}$–$10^{-1}$) are outside the oceanic calibration range — the $b=2f/r_{\rm eff}$ law still applies, but multiple scattering there is not a perturbation and the pedestal approximation degrades; flag dense-plume rendering as Monte-Carlo territory.

### 5. Bottom relief — non-flat bottom

**(i) Governing physics.**

*(a) Geometric self-shadowing.* A bottom point $\mathbf{x}_b$ with normal $\mathbf{N}_b$ receives direct (refracted) sun iff (1) it faces the refracted beam, $\mathbf{N}_b\!\cdot\!(-\mathbf{t})>0$ with $\mathbf{t}$ the *local* in-water sun direction (§1: $\theta_w\le48.6^\circ$ always), and (2) the straight segment from $\mathbf{x}_b$ back along $-\mathbf{t}$ to the surface is unobstructed by other bottom geometry. Because $\theta_w$ is bounded, shadow lengths are bounded too: a relief step of height $\Delta z$ casts a shadow of length $\Delta z\tan\theta_w\le1.13\,\Delta z$ along the solar azimuth — underwater relief shadows are *shorter and steeper* than the same scene in air at the same solar elevation, a distinctive look. Note the shadow-casting direction is $\mathbf{t}$, not the air sun direction and not the vertical: using either wrong direction is a common renderer bug with errors of order $D(\tan\theta_s-\tan\theta_w)$.

*(b) Caustics on slopes.* The transport map now ends on a surface $z=z_b(\mathbf{x})$: the intersection is implicit, $\mathbf{x}_b=\mathbf{x}_s+\ell\,\mathbf{t}$, $\ell$ solving $z_b(\mathbf{x}_s+\ell\mathbf{t}_{xy})=h(\mathbf{x}_s)+\ell t_z$. The bottom irradiance is

$$E_b=\frac{E_0\,T_F\,e^{-cL}}{|\det J_{\rm tot}|}\;\big(\mathbf{N}_b\!\cdot\!(-\mathbf{t})\big)\Big/\big(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\!\cdot\!(-\mathbf{t})\big)\text{-type projection factors},$$

i.e. three slope effects multiply: (1) **Lambert/projection**: flux per unit *bottom* area scales with $\cos$ of incidence on the bottom — a slope tilted toward the refracted sun receives up to $1/\cos\theta_w$ more per unit area than flat bottom (and slopes facing away receive zero direct, (a)); (2) **Jacobian change**: $J_{\rm tot}$ is the Jacobian of the map onto the *sloped* receiver — a bottom sloping down along the ray direction stretches the footprint (pattern diluted, lines widened), sloping up compresses it (pattern concentrated, lines narrowed and brightened), on top of the water-surface focusing; equivalently the effective propagation distance $L$ varies across the pattern, so different pattern components come into focus on a slope at different places than on a plane — the pattern is *displaced* by $\approx(\partial z_b)\tan\theta_w$ plus the $L$-change defocus; (3) **path-length variation**: $L(\mathbf{x})$ varies with relief, modulating $e^{-cL}$ across the pattern (a colour/brightness tilt in spectral rendering). Foreshortening in the *camera* view is a separate, purely projective effect handled by normal rendering.

*(c) Interreflection / bounce.* Bottom-reflected light re-enters the water, is partially total-internally-reflected at the surface (all upwelling directions beyond 48.6° from vertical are TIR-trapped), and re-illuminates the bottom — a diffuse cavity effect. Its size is governed by bottom albedo $A$: measured material reflectances for sand **0.2–0.7** (Mobley, shallow-water optics programme — who also showed multiple scattering *between sand ripples* cannot be ignored at these albedos, so the effective bottom BRDF is not the material BRDF), retrieved bottom albedos $\approx$24 % sand, 12–24 % coral, 12 % seagrass (Mishra et al. 2005); system-level, bright sand/turbid shallow water raises total surface albedo to $\approx$0.20 vs the 0.05 open-ocean value (Fogarty et al. 2018). Scaling: first-bounce pedestal $\sim A\times$(upwelling trapping fraction)$\times e^{-K_d\bar L}$ relative to direct — i.e. a $\sim$5–20 % diffuse fill in clear shallow water over bright sand, $<$2 % over dark bottoms/deeper water. It is *diffuse* (the bottom is rough, the surface return path is TIR-diffuse), so it reduces caustic contrast like the §4 pedestal but does **not** create second-generation caustics, except off genuinely specular objects (shells, metal, wet rock facets), which are a photon-mapping case, not a statistical term.

*(d) How production/research systems do it (cost/accuracy).*

| Method | How caustics reach 3-D geometry | Cost | Accuracy / failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forward photon mapping (Jensen 1996, *Rendering Techniques '96*; book 2001) | Photons from sun, refracted at surface, stored in a dedicated **caustic photon map** (LS+D paths only); density estimation at receiver points on *any* geometry | High: $10^6$–$10^8$ photons for clean patterns; memory-bound; progressive photon mapping (Hachisuka & Jensen 2008) removes memory limit | Reference quality incl. shadows, volume; biased by density-estimation kernel (blur vs noise trade-off); the ground truth other methods approximate |
| Backward/gather analytic Jacobian (this renderer's family; Premož & Ashikhmin 2000 beam tracing is a relative) | Per receiver point, invert map to surface, evaluate $\|\det J\|^{-1}$ (+ sum over pre-images, §0) | Low–moderate: one surface solve per receiver sample; no storage | Exact for single-refraction direct caustics on arbitrary receivers *if* multi-root handled; no bounce, no volume without extra terms (§4); caustics *of* objects (not just of the water surface) not included |
| Caustic map / image-space (Wyman & Davis 2006; Shah, Konttinen & Pattanaik 2007, *IEEE TVCG* 13:272) | Photons splatted in light space into a caustic texture; Shah et al. iterate receiver position against a light-space depth map → works over real bathymetry (vs plane-only predecessors) | Real-time; texture-resolution-bound | Aliasing/leaks at silhouettes and steep relief; resolution must cover receiver extent; approximate energy |
| Guardado & Sánchez-Crespo 2004 (GPU Gems 2, Ch. 2) | Backward single ray per floor vertex to a sun-disc texture | Cheapest | Assumes noon sun + (near-)flat floor by construction; "aesthetics-driven", authors' own framing; only $\sim$1–5 % of rays in true forward/backward tracing are "useful" is their motivation — but the approximation is not a transport solution; do not use as v4 baseline |
| Yuksel & Keyser heightfield caustics | Two-pass backward gathering over a *neighbourhood* of surface points per receiver point | Real-time, much better than single-point | Heightfield receivers; neighbourhood search cost grows with depth/curvature |
| Texture-space / lightmap baking (production film/games practice) | Any of the above rendered into the receiver's UV/lightmap, cached per frame/time-step | Amortised; memory = lightmap size | Temporal reuse artefacts; fails for deforming receivers; the practical choice for offline rendering with animated water — recompute per time sample, not per camera frame |

Premož & Ashikhmin (2000, "Rendering Natural Waters", EGWR) remain the template for combining oceanographic spectra + full Fresnel + in-water scattering ($c=a+b_b$ formalism) with caustic imagery in one system.

**(ii) Independent vs derived.** Bottom heightfield/BRDF are **independent scene inputs**. Shadowing, slope-Jacobian, displacement, path-length variation are **derived** — they must come out of intersecting the *same* transport rays with the heightfield; a separately painted "slope darkening" would double-count the Lambert term already implicit in a correct $J_{\rm tot}$. Bounce is derived in magnitude from $A$ and $K_d$ but its *spatial distribution* is an approximation choice (uniform pedestal vs radiosity/photon bounce).

**(iii) Implementation route.** New machinery, in order: (1) **Heightfield bottom as transport receiver**: replace the plane intersection with heightfield ray-marching/Newton iteration per transport ray (the map is smooth; 2–3 iterations from the plane solution suffice except at grazing relief). (2) **Visibility/shadow pass**: for each bottom sample, trace $-\mathbf{t}$ to the surface against the heightfield (a heightfield shadow-map in the *refracted* light direction, or ray-march in the same pass as (1) — the segment surface→bottom is already computed, so test it against $z_b$: cost $\approx$ one extra heightfield traversal per sample). This replaces — for the bottom — any reliance on the statistical Smith/Beckmann term for cast shadows (that term stays valid only for surface microfacet shading). (3) **Slope-corrected Jacobian**: compute $J_{\rm tot}$ including receiver-plane tilt (automatic if $J$ is evaluated by finite differences of the *actual* intersected map $\Phi$, which is the robust way: perturb $\mathbf{x}_s$, re-intersect, difference $\mathbf{x}_b$ in bottom-tangent coordinates). (4) **Optional bounce pedestal**: one diffuse term $\propto A\,E_{\rm up}\,e^{-K_d L}$ with $E_{\rm up}$ from the bottom-averaged upwelling — one global (or tiled) scalar per frame, *not* a radiosity solve; upgrade path is a single bounce in a photon pass if §5(d) photon mapping is ever adopted. (5) Photo-texture stays, but must be treated as albedo (multiplied by total irradiance incl. caustic ratio, shadow, pedestal), with the heightfield's geometric normal — not the texture's — driving the Lambert term.

**(iv) Honest limits.** Heightfield $\ne$ geometry: overhangs, caves, and object undersides need mesh receivers (the Jacobian-by-finite-difference formulation generalises, the heightfield intersector does not). Bottom BRDFs in nature are non-Lambertian (sand ripple multiple scattering, Mobley) — a Lambertian assumption misestimates grazing-view brightness; treat ripple-scale BRDF as calibration. The bounce pedestal's trapping fraction depends on the full angular upwelling distribution (Hydrolight territory); the 5–20 % range above is an estimate band, not a derivation. Caustics cast *by* objects (a glass float, a rock's specular highlight) are outside surface-Jacobian transport entirely.

### 6. Shadows as a first-class element

**(i) Governing physics.**

*The dark complement is already in the Jacobian.* Energy conservation over the map, $\int_{\rm bottom}E_b\,dA=\int_{\rm surface}T_FE_0\,dA$ (minus attenuation), forces bright folds to be *paid for* by defocused regions: where $\det J>1$ the same formula gives $E_b<\bar E$ — broad, soft dark lanes interleaved with the bright network. These are not shadows of anything; they are the caustic field's negative. A renderer whose "caustic ratio" is clamped to $\ge1$ or normalised to preserve the *pattern's* mean incorrectly will silently violate conservation and over-brighten the scene; conversely a correct ratio field has spatial mean $=T_F e^{-c\bar L}$ (times direct fraction), with dark regions going to $\sim$0.3–0.6 of mean in typical fields and toward 0 inside geometric shadow boundaries of folds (the Airy dark side). Stramski's flash definition ($1.5\times$) and the measured minima (e.g. $E_d$ down to 29 % of mean at 7 m, clear sky, Hieronymi & Macke) characterise both sides of this distribution.

*Cast shadows through a wavy surface.* An occluder (object in the column, surface-piercing object, or bottom relief, §5) blocks the sun for a set of *surface entry points*. The shadow on the bottom is that blocked set transported by the same map $\Phi$: hence (a) the shadow boundary is **displaced, stretched and locally re-focused** by the Jacobian — shadow edges can be *sharpened* where $\det J<1$ (the map compresses the penumbra) and *smeared/fragmented* where $\det J>1$ or where the map folds (a shadow edge crossing a caustic fold is duplicated/torn, because the receiver there has 3 pre-images, some shadowed, some not — this is the physical origin of "shadow flicker" and of bright caustic lines appearing *inside* geometric shadows); (b) the shadow of a *surface-piercing* occluder is additionally modulated because the occluder perturbs the wave field itself (out of scope for a spectrum renderer — flag, don't model).

*Penumbra: sun disc vs wave slopes — the key ordering.* The sun is an extended source, angular diameter 0.53° (Guardado & Sánchez-Crespo's geometry: 1.42 M km at 147–152 M km). Refraction compresses angular size by $d\theta_w/d\theta_s=\cos\theta_s/(n\cos\theta_w)$: at noon the in-water disc is $\approx$0.40° across ($7\times10^{-3}$ rad). Geometric penumbra width for an occluder a distance $\ell$ above the receiver: $w_{\rm pen}\approx\ell\times7\times10^{-3}\approx7$ mm per metre at noon — *small*. Wave-slope smearing is usually **much larger**: the refracted direction varies across facets by $\sim(1-1/n)\times$slope angle, and Cox–Munk rms slopes are $\sqrt{0.003+0.00512U}\approx$ 0.10 rad (5.7°) at $U=2$ m s$^{-1}$, 0.17 rad ($\approx$10°) at 5 m s$^{-1}$, 0.24 rad at 10 m s$^{-1}$ — i.e. shadow-edge jitter of order $\ell\times0.02$–0.06 even after the $(1-1/n)$ factor, several times the sun-disc penumbra, and *time-varying* at wave frequencies. Conclusions: (1) at glassy calm the sun disc sets shadow softness and must be modelled (it also sets the caustic regularisation, §0 — same kernel, use it consistently); (2) in any real sea state, shadow breakup is a *transport* phenomenon (the map applied to the visibility function), not a blur radius; a renderer that softens shadows with a screen-space blur will get calm scenes wrong (too soft if tuned for waves) and wavy scenes wrong (uniformly soft instead of torn/flickering). (3) The existing Smith/Beckmann statistical term models the probability that a *microfacet* is shadowed by neighbouring microfacets at the water surface for direct-light shading of the surface itself — it says nothing about cast shadows on the bottom and must not be presented as the shadow solution.

*Unified treatment.* One pass suffices conceptually: bottom direct irradiance

$$E_{\rm dir}(\mathbf{x}_b)=E_0\,f_{\rm dir}\sum_{\text{pre-images }i}\frac{T_F(\theta_i^{(i)})\,V\!\big(\mathbf{x}_s^{(i)}\big)\,e^{-cL_i}}{|\det J_i|}\;\big(\mathbf{N}_b\!\cdot\!(-\mathbf{t}_i)\big),$$

where $V(\mathbf{x}_s)\in[0,1]$ is the sun-disc-integrated visibility of the surface entry point (occluders between sun and surface, and — applied along the in-water segment — occluders between surface and bottom, §5). Caustics ($|\det J|^{-1}$), defocus darkening ($\det J>1$), cast shadows ($V$), Fresnel, and attenuation appear as factors of a single sum over the same pre-images; shadow flicker, caustics-inside-shadows (partially shadowed pre-image sets), and sharpened shadow edges all fall out. Diffuse/ambient and pedestal terms (§4–5) are *added after*, unshadowed by $V$ only insofar as they are truly diffuse (a large occluder does shadow the diffuse field too — second order).

**(ii) Independent vs derived.** Occluder geometry is an **independent input**. $V$ as a *function on the surface*, its transport, penumbra, flicker spectrum, and the dark-complement field are all **derived**. The sun-disc angular size is a known constant (0.53°, with the $\pm$1.7 % annual distance variation if anyone cares), not a calibration knob; "shadow softness" must not be an independent artistic parameter — it is $\ell\cdot\Delta\theta_{w,\odot}$ plus map distortion.

**(iii) Implementation route.** New machinery: (1) **Visibility pass in sun space**: render/sample occluders from the sun into a shadow map *on the water surface's parameterisation* (entry-point $V$), integrating the sun disc by jittering over the (refracted) disc or by percentage-closer soft sampling with radius set by $\Delta\theta_{w,\odot}\times$ occluder-to-surface distance — note $V$ lives at the *surface*, so its resolution requirement is set by occluder size at the surface, not by bottom pattern frequency (a major cost saving vs bottom-space shadow maps). (2) **In-water segment visibility** for submerged occluders and bottom relief: reuse the §5 heightfield/object intersection along each transport ray (this is $V$ applied on the second segment; implement $V=V_{\rm above}\times V_{\rm below}$). (3) Multiply into the existing Jacobian sum per pre-image — *per pre-image*, not per bottom point, or folded regions will be wrong. (4) Retire any $\max(\text{ratio},1)$-type clamps; validate with the §0 energy check *including* a shadow test scene (shadowed area must be compensated by brightening elsewhere only through the map, not globally). Cost: one extra shadow-map pass + per-ray segment test; no photon machinery required for sun shadows specifically.

**(iv) Honest limits.** $V$ from a shadow map is a sampling approximation: thin occluders (rigging, vegetation) alias. Shadows of the *diffuse* sky component and of the §4/§5 pedestals are neglected by the factorised form (acceptable: diffuse shadows are low-contrast by nature, but in very shallow water under a large occluder — a pier — the error is visible and would need an ambient-occlusion-like term, which is then a calibration, not transport). Wave–occluder interaction (a pier's own wave shadow/diffraction of the *water waves*) is real physics outside a prescribed-spectrum renderer. Finally, the flicker *spectrum* of a shadow edge is a derived diagnostic of the same kind as §0: if validation needs it, compare against the wave-period/half-period benchmarks rather than tuning it directly.

## Could not verify

- No source, in ocean optics or rendering, quantifies a **direct** contribution of airborne spray/splash droplets to bottom caustics relative to wave caustics (§3) — treated above as a genuine negative result with the indirect mechanisms quantified instead.
- Stramski & Tegowski (2001) full text was not opened (paywalled); its quantitative claims are taken from the EarthRef abstract record and Stramski's 2024 synthesis slides, which agree with Terrill et al. (2001), opened in full.
- Mobley's *Light and Water* (1994) itself is not openly available online; Mobley-attributed values used here come from the shallow-water optics programme report (DTIC) and Preisendorfer–Mobley as cited by Hieronymi & Macke.
- Exact in-water sun-disc penumbra and footprint-stretch factors are derived here from Snell/Fresnel geometry (shown with formulas) rather than quoted from a single measurement paper; no published measurement series of "caustic anisotropy vs solar zenith" as such was found — the anisotropy claims follow from the map geometry plus the Gernez/Hieronymi result that normalised CV is nearly sun-altitude-independent.
- Dense-plume void fractions (0.3–0.4) come from whitecap-plume measurement summaries (secondary reporting of Deane/Melville programme data), not from an opened primary table; the oceanic dilute-regime numbers are from opened primary sources.
