# RALLY-V4-3-1 adversarial review — C1–C10 verdicts

ROLE-ACK: adversarial reviewer. Attack, do not approve. Settled rulings (v3.6 a/b split, v16 FF-CDF halo 5/5, v4-1.8/v4-1.9 deposit + disc quadrature, four-way GRIN+RTE ruling, v5 salinity gate, pond-thermal ruling, no-CFD / no-KH / no-double-diffusion rulings) are NOT re-litigated.

## C1. GRIN bending matters on sun side as well as view side
**Verdict: STANDS**
**Strongest counterargument:** The ray equation is time-symmetric, but the observable is not: the sun has ~9.3 mrad angular diameter while schlieren deflections here are ~1 mrad. Sun-disc blur (already in the stack via disc quadrature) can wash out sun-side deposit shifts while view-side apparent shifts survive, making a view-only implementation look sufficient at the main size. A one-sided design would then pass a loose band-energy test while failing a filament-position test.
**Failure condition:** Kills C1 if ray-traced A/B comparison (GRIN on vs off, collimated vs disc sun) shows sun-side deposit centroid/filament shift < 0.5 pixel at the main size (1.4755 m × 1.0828 m window, 0.6 m depth) while view-side shift remains > 1 pixel, across the full bed-heating ΔT range. Conversely C1 is confirmed if sun-side filament displacement alone exceeds pixel threshold or changes line-depth statistics beyond tolerance.

## C2. Bed-heating plume displacement 1–4 mm (>pixel); cool-skin-only is sub-pixel and correctly deprioritised
**Verdict: STANDS**
**Strongest counterargument:** The 1–4 mm band inherits ΔT from the surface energy balance and a plume width of order depth. If realised ΔT is at the low end (well-mixed 0.6 m pond, windy cool skin renewing fast, spring through-flow flushing heat) the band collapses toward ≤0.5 mm. In the other direction, cool-skin dismissal assumes a thin horizontal layer at near-normal incidence gives negligible lateral bend — but under oblique sun/view rays and wind-roughened micro-gradients the integrated ∇n through the skin plus the first cm of cool water can approach pixel scale as shimmer, not as mean shift. Both ends of the band are model-sensitive, not measured.
**Failure condition:** Kills C2 if (a) measured or energy-balance-constrained ΔT at the main size yields ray-traced displacement < 1 pixel for >90% of rays/frames, or (b) a cool-skin + wind-renewal micro-profile yields ray-traced apparent shimmer ≥ 1 pixel. C2 stands if the 1–4 mm derivation reproduces within factor ~2 in a blind ray trace with no tuned coefficients.

## C3. Parametrised plume field is admissible as "labelled input" without a convection solver
**Verdict: STANDS**
**Strongest counterargument:** "Same status as secondary-flow surface patterns" is precedent, not physics. A parametrised (position/spacing/ΔT) field is admissible under (b) "corresponds to actual physics" only while every knob is pinned outside the image: spacing from stability scale, ΔT from energy balance, positions from bed albedo/spring maps. The moment any of them is adjusted to make caustics "look natural," it becomes a look-approximation violating the governing principle, and without a solver there is no internal dynamical consistency check to catch it.
**Failure condition:** Kills C3 if any plume parameter is fitted to image goodness (caustic sharpness, line-depth match) rather than to independent thermal/boundary inputs, or if two parameter sets with identical energy-balance inputs give caustic statistics differing beyond verification tolerance (non-identifiability). C3 stands while all three parameter families are traceable to non-image measurements/inputs with stated uncertainties.

## C4. Inhomogeneous RTE needs no new kernel shape; local (τ, b/c) evaluation of v16 FF-CDF suffices
**Verdict: WEAKENED**
**Strongest counterargument:** Local evaluation is the small-gradient limit. It fails in exactly the cases stage 3 exists for: (i) sharp lateral gradients (plume edges, inflow-turbidity fronts, brackish fronts) where lateral transport makes the halo non-local — the radiance at x depends on b/c metres away, not at x; (ii) mixed populations — CDOM absorbs but does not scatter, mineral sediment vs organics vs bubbles have different phase-function shapes, so scaling one FF shape by local b/c is wrong in kind, not degree. Bubbles (stage 2-supplied concentration) are the sharpest counterexample: large-sphere Mie/air-film phase functions are not FF-sediment. What must change is then the kernel mixture/shape, not only bookkeeping.
**Failure condition:** Kills C4 as stated if delta-tracking MC on a sharp-front or two-population field (sediment+CDOM, or sediment+bubbles) shows the halo containment curve or band energies cannot be matched by any local-(τ,b/c) FF-CDF evaluation within the v16 tolerance, while a mixed-kernel or non-local treatment can. C4 survives only with an explicit domain restriction: smooth single-population fields with gradient scale ≫ 1/c and ≫ halo width.

## C5. Path-radiance (veil) term can be built now against fixed top-down view; stage 4 only re-points the eye end
**Verdict: STANDS**
**Strongest counterargument:** "Only re-pointing" understates the coupling: veil is ∫ source × transmittance along the in-water LOS, and the LOS entry point/direction is set by camera-dependent refraction at the wavy surface (Snell + Fresnel with spatially varying sub-surface n) plus in-water scattering-angle sampling. A top-down implementation that hard-codes vertical LOS, orthographic veil export, or angle-averaged surface transmission bakes in the very assumption stage 4 removes. Polarisation/Fresnel angular dependence at oblique views then forces a rewrite, not a re-point.
**Failure condition:** Kills C5 if the stage-3 veil code cannot produce an oblique-LOS veil by changing only ray origin/direction + surface-intersection (i.e. requires changing the source-function integral, surface-transmission model, or export geometry). C5 stands if the veil is implemented from day one as a general LOS integral with the top-down view as one input case and the separated-veil export in geometry-independent units.

## C6. Delta-tracking MC is the correct reference; HydroLight-class (plane-parallel) cannot serve
**Verdict: STANDS**
**Strongest counterargument:** Delta-tracking verifies numerics against itself, not physics against nature: with the same a(x), b(x), FF inputs fed to both sides, agreement proves bookkeeping, not that the inputs are right. HydroLight, for all its plane-parallel/finite-patch limitations, is anchored to ocean-optics closure data; discarding it entirely throws away the only field-validated anchor for the homogeneous limit. MC non-convergence/noise on small grids can also masquerade as physics disagreement.
**Failure condition:** Kills C6 if (a) small-grid Woodcock MC fails to reproduce v16 homogeneous numbers within stated statistical error (reference itself untrustworthy), or (b) a finite-bottom/patched plane-parallel configuration is shown to reproduce our main-size geometry within tolerance (then HydroLight-class could serve as a cross-anchor). C6 stands while MC reproduces (i) the homogeneous limit and (ii) analytic τ in the uniform case, with reported variance, and HydroLight is retained only as a homogeneous-limit sanity anchor, not the 3-D reference.

## C7. Single scattering suffices at cL ≈ 0.2; must be decided by order-capped vs uncapped MC, not taste
**Verdict: STANDS**
**Strongest counterargument:** Energy fraction is not image-formation fraction: at τ = 0.2, second-order energy is ~τ²/2 ≈ 2%, but in dark caustic shadows / dense veil regions the multiply-scattered pedestal is a large fraction of local signal and sets line-depth contrast — the project's own diagnostic. Order-capped comparison on the same fields also shares phase-function/field errors, so it decides internal consistency, not absolute error vs nature. Taste is removed; shared-blindness is not.
**Failure condition:** Kills the "adequate at 0.2" half if order-capped (≤1) vs uncapped MC at cL = 0.2 differs by more than the gate tolerances on band energy, halo containment, line-depth distribution, or separated veil structure. Kills the "first-order at 1+" half if cL = 1–2 sweeps still show differences within tolerance (then H5 removal is unnecessary). C7 stands if the 0.2/0.5/1/2 sweep draws the boundary by numbers with pre-registered tolerances.

## C8. Halocline as sharp step; thermal plume as continuous GRIN — split is physical, not arbitrary
**Verdict: WEAKENED**
**Strongest counterargument:** Real haloclines at 0.6 m creek scale are not zero-thickness steps: entrainment — the design's own closure — creates a finite-thickness interfacial/transition layer with continuous S(z) (and hence n(z)) plus interleaving from wind/strain and bed friction. A pure Snell-step inserts a spurious sharp TIR edge and misplaces the deflection-angle distribution, while the continuous GRIN integrator of 3.1 already handles arbitrary n(z). Salt diffuses ~100× slower than heat, so the step direction is right, but zero-thickness overstates it; the correct object is step-plus-mixed-layer, with thickness from the entrainment state.
**Failure condition:** Kills pure-step C8 if measured S(z)/n(z) micro-profiles across a 0.6 m-class front show transition thickness comparable to scattering length 1/c, halo width, or plume-gradient scale, and ray traces show step vs resolved-gradient differ by > tolerance on deflection-angle map or deposit bands. C8 stands in full only with a thickness criterion (step iff transition ≪ 1/c and ≪ optical gradient scale; else resolved layer via 3.1).

## C9. E(Ri) entrainment closure transfers flume → 0.6 m creek without modification (Dolcetti-stage-1 precedent)
**Verdict: WEAKENED**
**Strongest counterargument:** E(Ri) dimensionless collapse holds in fully turbulent, high-Re, smooth-flume overflows; 0.6 m creek scenes add low-Re/transitional patches, bed roughness, vegetation/detritus, wind stress, and unsteady inflow — all Ri-independent modifiers of E. "Without modification" plus analogy to Dolcetti is precedent-stacking: Dolcetti transfer was itself a measured-scale claim requiring validation, not a licence. Turner (1986) compilations show order-of-magnitude scatter in E at fixed Ri across facilities; picking the curve without an in-situ scale factor is taste disguised as literature.
**Failure condition:** Kills "without modification" if 0.6 m-class front-speed (≈√(g′h₁)), convergence (≈0.05 s⁻¹ range), or downwelling (0.2–0.35 m/s) reproduction misses stated ranges with literature E, or requires a Re/roughness correction or fitted prefactor. C9 stands in full only if those three checks pass with E taken directly from Turner with no post-hoc tuning; otherwise it survives as "same functional family, site prefactor measured in 3.4."

## C10. No full 3-D volume PDE solver needed; reduced-model fields (n,a,b)(x,t) + exact transport suffices
**Verdict: WEAKENED**
**Strongest counterargument:** Caustics depend on curvature (second derivatives) and space-time correlations of n, not on means: two fields with identical reduced parameters (same ΔT, same layer thicknesses, same mean τ) but different intermittency/spectra advected by real flow give different filament networks and different line-depth/pattern-discriminator scores. Reduced models supply climatology; image formation samples weather. Without any advection-diffusion consistency (even kinematic), plume-rise/front-motion timing over the 8 s loop and particle-settling/CDOM-strain histories are imposed by hand and can be tuned to the image — a direct governing-principle risk. The "no-CFD" ruling forbids a full solver; it does not prove the remainder is sufficient.
**Failure condition:** Kills C10 if, at fixed reduced parameters, an ensemble of physically admissible realisations (different plume phases/intermittency, different sub-grid spectra, or kinematic advection vs frozen field) spreads caustic gates (energy, line-depth, pattern discriminator, deflection/τ maps) beyond tolerance — i.e. means do not determine the image. C10 stands only with a demonstrated insensitivity result (or bounded uncertainty) at all three verification sizes, plus a loop-closure proof that periodic-boundary renewal does not inject artificial 8 s periodicity into those gates.

## MISSED ENTIRELY
1. **Wavy-surface ↔ volume refraction coupling (belongs to 3.1 + stage-1 interface).** Why it matters: every bent path refracts at the moving stage-1 surface via Snell/Fresnel with sub-surface n(x) varying (T/S), not scene-constant n; TIR and Fresnel reflectance on the view side depend on that local n and on incidence set by the camera. Designing GRIN paths against a flat/fixed surface and adding stage-1 finish later misplaces both deposit (sun entry) and veil (eye exit). Goes to 3.1 (entry/exit refraction with local n) and 3.4 (joint surface+volume verification case).
2. **Dispersion: n(S,T,λ) chromatic bending (belongs to 3.1).** Why it matters: Quan & Fry is spectral; schlieren deflection is λ-dependent, producing chromatic fringing of caustic filaments. A single-n or RGB-lumped integrator meets band-energy gates while failing spectral line-depth/pattern physics. Goes to 3.1 (spectral ray integration or bounded achromatic error proof) and 3.4 (spectral filament check).
3. **Bubble / large-particle phase-function mixture (belongs to 3.2).** Why it matters: stage 3 consumes a stage-2 bubble concentration if supplied; bubbles and flocs are not FF-sediment — evaluating the v16 FF-CDF at local b/c mis-shapes forward-peak and halo while conserving energy, directly biasing containment curves. Goes to 3.2 (mixture kernel: FF-sediment + Mie/bubble term keyed to bubble field; freshwater-pond default may be zero-bubble but the interface must exist).
4. **Inelastic volumetric sources: CDOM fluorescence + Raman (belongs to 3.2).** Why it matters: CDOM is carried as absorption a(x), but UV→visible fluorescence and water Raman are real in-water photon sources with different spectra/geometry than elastic veil; in clear-pond blue-green bands they offset veil colour and shadow fill. Omitting them is a physics omission, not numerics. Goes to 3.2 (either include as spectral source terms or bound them below gate tolerance with measurements).
5. **Particle settling / flocculation / resuspension on the 8 s timescale + inflow-plume buoyancy (belongs to 3.2 + 3.3 + 3.4).** Why it matters: a(x),b(x) are not passive tracers — coarse sediment settles, fines flocculate, bed shear resuspends, turbid inflows are themselves buoyant (freshwater analogue in 3.3). Frozen/statistically-stationary renewal with periodic boundaries can hold the wrong vertical profile for the whole loop. Goes to 3.2 (settling velocity per class), 3.3 (buoyant inflow coupling), 3.4 (profile-evolution check over 8 s).
6. **Sub-surface n sun-entry boundary condition + sky/sun angular structure through waves (belongs to 3.1 + stage-1 coupling, verified in 3.4).** Why it matters: deposit position depends on directional sun entry through wave slopes; volume GRIN without joint sun–surface–volume ray continuity double-counts or drops slope-induced entry-angle spread. Goes to 3.4 as an explicit joint verification scene (flat vs wavy entry with GRIN on).
7. **In-situ n(x)/T(x) and (a,b)(x) maps at verification sizes — the measurement gap (belongs to 3.4).** Why it matters: every 3.1–3.3 verification listed is limit-check or model-vs-MC (self-consistency). No BOS/schlieren-imaged T-field, no micro-thermistor/S-probe transect, no AC-S-type a/b transect at main/sub/contrast sizes is tasked — so the chain is verified against itself, never against nature, violating the project's own measurement standard. Goes to 3.4 (minimum transect/BOS dataset or explicit uncertainty bound carried into every gate).
8. **Loop-closure periodicity proof for the 8 s loop (belongs to 3.4).** Why it matters: "generation with periodic boundaries" is permitted only if it does not bend field physics; plume-rise times, front transit, eddy renewal and settling times are mostly ≠ 8 s or divisors thereof, so naive tiling injects detectable periodicity into caustic dynamics. Goes to 3.4 (autocorrelation/periodicity gate on caustic time series, not only on fields).

FINAL VERDICT: STANDS — the four-block shape holds, but C4/C8/C9/C10 survive only in the restricted forms above and misses 1–8 must be tasked inside 3.1–3.4; no new block needed.
