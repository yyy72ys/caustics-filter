# RALLY-V4-4-1 adversarial review — camera/lens stage decomposition + C1–C9

## Per-claim verdicts (C1–C9)

**C1 — VERDICT: STANDS. Strongest counterargument:** The claim is right to forbid pre-blur substitution, but its positive prescription — "aperture integral over the post-transport radiance field" — is underspecified to the point of being unimplementable as written. A 2-D post-transport radiance image (one RGB value + one displacement per pixel, computed for a pinhole/chief ray) does not contain the angular light-field needed for a finite-aperture integral. A finite aperture means N pupil samples refract at N different water-surface points with N different normals, spawn N different caustic topologies, and see different occlusions/parallax of 3-D bed relief. Integrating a fixed 2-D image with a per-pixel CoC kernel is still a 2-D convolution, i.e. a sophisticated pre-blur in disguise — it commutes blur and transport, which is exactly what the claim forbids. Caustic formation is nonlinear in surface slope/curvature: transport-after-blur ≠ blur-after-transport even for a fronto-parallel plane, because smoothing the C-field/displacement first destroys the fold catastrophes that create filaments, while blurring afterward merely softens them. So C1's prohibition stands, but v4-4.3 as described does not yet satisfy it. **Failure condition:** aperture diameter comparable to caustic cell size or to wave-slope correlation length; oblique view or high-relief bed where marginal pupil rays intersect a different wave facet than the chief ray; quantitative test — compare (a) per-pixel CoC convolution of the pinhole render vs (b) full pupil re-trace through the live water surface for f/2.8, 35 mm, close-focus 1.0 m: if filament peak intensity / filament width differ by >10% or filament topology changes (merge/split), the 2-D integral implementation fails and C1 is violated by the implementation itself.

**C2 — VERDICT: STANDS. Strongest counterargument:** Paraxial `apparent depth = real depth / n` is textbook-correct for a flat interface at nadir, and using geometric distance would indeed miss focus by ~33% (0.60 m → 0.45 m apparent in the worked example). But "the apparent-distance field per pixel" implies one scalar depth per pixel, which does not exist once the interface is wavy and the aperture is finite. Each object point has (i) a chief-ray virtual image, (ii) a 2-D astigmatic interval (sagittal vs tangential foci split), (iii) a pupil-dependent spread because each aperture sub-ray sees a different local tilt/curvature, and (iv) time dependence at ripple frequencies — the field must be re-evaluated per frame of the 8 s loop, not baked once. Plane-refraction calibration against flat water therefore proves almost nothing about the wavy case that actually generates caustics. Worse, the "object" is a diffuse bed seen through a dynamic lens (the water surface itself): the water surface already images the bed before the camera lens does, including multiple virtual images near fold caustics in image space. A single apparent-depth scalar linearises away that first imaging step. **Failure condition:** wave slope ≥10–15° or incidence ≥20° from nadir, where the paraxial 1/n rule errs by >5–10% and sagittal–tangential split exceeds the depth of field at f/2.8 (see C9); or any frame where one bed point maps to two virtual images (surface-curvature-induced doubling) — no single `s(x,y)` can focus both, focus becomes undefined and the v4-4.2 contract breaks.

**C3 — VERDICT: STANDS. Strongest counterargument:** The no-stacking conclusion is correct, but the premise — "E=2.2 and A=2.6 are recording-side constants" — is a relabelling, not a physical identification. A real recording chain (shutter time × aperture area × gain × QE(λ)) is linear and common to all photons; it cannot implement "gain on the direct term only" (E) nor a scene-dependent ambient rescale (A). Those numbers smell like transport-compensation fudge: missing sky/multiple-scattering energy (A) and a look-tuned highlight balance (E), both absorbing errors from stages 2–3. Calling them "recording-side" therefore launders a transport error into the camera. Neither REINTERPRET nor REPLACE is clean until that contamination is measured: reinterpretation forces a non-physical per-path gain into the camera model; replacement silently discards the error compensation and breaks every preset that was approved under the frozen look. **Failure condition:** radiometric test — render a calibrated diffuse grey wedge + flat-water analytic reference with E/A stripped vs retained: if removing them changes large-area mean by ≠ uniform scale (e.g. direct vs ambient ratio shifts, shadows go black while highlights clip), they were never pure exposure and stacking a physical exposure model on top double-counts (highlights blow out by ~2.2×) or hollows shadows (ambient drops by 2.6×).

**C4 — VERDICT: WEAKENED. Strongest counterargument:** Thin-lens + geometric `b = (f²/N)·|s−s_f|/(s_f·(s−f))` + Airy `2.44·λ·N` is the correct paraxial v1 baseline, but "sufficient, absence of aberrations within PSF-shape verification error" is unfalsifiable because no lens, no field position, and no tolerance are named. For high-contrast caustic filaments the PSF wings — not FWHM — set visible contrast, and real lenses depart from Gaussian+Airy exactly there: spherical aberration (asymmetric through-focus), coma/field curvature off-axis (sub preset: 4.8 m wide frame), longitudinal/lateral chromatic aberration (filament fringing), and veiling glare. Composition is also wrong as stated: defocused diffraction is not `geometric kernel ⊕ Airy disc`; it is a depth-dependent diffraction integral (Hopkins/Strehl family) where at f/16 the Airy disc (~21 µm, ~3.6 px at 5.9 µm pitch) already exceeds the geometric CoC criterion, so "in focus" by geometry is out of focus by wave optics. A generic "PSF-shape verification" without a pass/fail MTF mask at caustic spatial frequencies proves nothing. **Failure condition:** fast aperture f/1.4–f/2.8 on-axis (aberrations ≫ diffraction) or any off-axis point in the sub/contrast presets where measured sagittal/meridional MTF at the caustic-web Nyquist (cell 1/20–1/5 frame width → ~100–400 px period, filament core few px) deviates from the thin-lens+Airy prediction by >20%, or saturated filament shows colour fringing / asymmetric flare the model cannot produce.

**C5 — VERDICT: STANDS. Strongest counterargument:** The physics/convention split is principled — stage 4 should output physical blur diameter (mm or px), PJ9/display owns the viewing-condition threshold — but the separation leaks operationally. Every actionable decision inside stage 4 (aperture choice in v4-4.3, pull visibility in v4-4.4, regime map in C7, "bed enters at f/16") smuggles a threshold back in. Without one, "focus pull exists" is undefined: the same file is simultaneously "sharp" on a phone and "soft" pixel-peeping the 2048² master. Sensor pixel pitch (5.9 µm in the example) and diffraction already are physical thresholds inside stage 4 — if `b < pitch` or `b < Airy`, the optical distinction is unrecordable — so stage 4 cannot be threshold-free. The risk is bureaucratic: outputting raw diameters without a reference threshold exports the pass/fail criterion to a project (PJ9) that does not own the lens physics. **Failure condition:** evaluation flip — bed blur 0.139 mm ≈ 4.8× CoC(d/1730) reads as "hopelessly out" under d/1730 but "acceptable" under a coarse video/large-viewing-distance CoC, or invisible after 2048²→1080p downscale + loop compression; any verification that passes/fails the same render depending on undisclosed display assumption fails C5's interface contract.

**C6 — VERDICT: WEAKENED. Strongest counterargument:** The scoping decision (exclude breathing in v1) is defensible for a locked-off scientific camera, but the physics justification overreaches. Part of breathing — paraxial magnification change `m = f/(s−f)`, FOV rescaling with `s_f`, chief-ray/W recalibration `W = W_s·(s/f−1)` — IS derivable from a thin lens and therefore cannot be declared "underivable." What is lens-specific is the residual: pupil magnification, distortion breathing, and non-ideal `f_eff(s_f)` nonlinearity. Excluding even the thin-lens component while performing a focus pull `s_f(t)` (surface→bed) introduces a systematic scale error that breaks the loop-closure and preset back-computation the design demands: frame edges drift by the magnification delta each pull cycle. An external `f_eff(s_f)` table alone does not fix distortion/FOV coupling. **Failure condition:** close-focus pull 1.0 m (surface) → 1.45 m apparent (bed) at 35 mm where Δm/m ≈ several % → multi-pixel edge displacement on 2048² (≫1 px) visible as breathing judder at the 8 s loop seam `s_f(0)=s_f(T)`; if edge registration error exceeds 1 px or preset inversion `frame→camera params` becomes non-unique once `f` is allowed to vary, the "exclude" clause fails as a closed specification.

**C7 — VERDICT: WEAKENED. Strongest counterargument:** The arithmetic checks out (hyperfocal `H=f²/(N·c)+f` with f=35 mm, c=0.029 mm: H≈15.1 m at f/2.8, ≈5.28 m at f/8, ≈2.64 m at f/16; quoted near/far ranges 0.94–1.07 m, 0.85–1.23 m, 0.73–1.59 m, and 1.92–6.90 m at 3.0 m all reproduce), and the meta-point — DoF/pull are regime-dependent, ship the map or ship a no-op — is the strongest process claim in the package. But the optical criterion behind the headline ("at f/16 the bed enters") collapses under wave optics and MTF: at f/16 the Airy disc ≈21 µm ≈ 3.6 px at 5.9 µm pitch already exceeds d/1730 economics, so geometric "in focus" means contrast-destroyed at filament frequencies. The calculation also substitutes apparent depth into an air-only DoF formula without the NA correction for imaging through a refractive plane (effective F-number for the submerged object differs), assumes one CoC/d/1730 for all three presets and all delivery scales, ignores the C9 astigmatic interval (which can exceed geometric DoF), and ignores temporal/loop visibility (pull perceptibility depends on caustic motion + compression, not static CoC). The map is mandatory, but this map — single-CoC, geometric-only, single-preset — is the wrong map. **Failure condition:** MTF-aware test — slanted-edge or filament-contrast measurement at the caustic core frequency shows f/16 "in-focus" bed contrast < f/8 "out-of-focus" bed contrast (diffraction wins); or sub preset (4.8 m frame, 4.0 m depth) / oblique shot where the quoted 1.0 m-height numbers mis-predict the visible-pull boundary by >1 stop; either breaks the claim as a shipping criterion.

**C8 — VERDICT: STANDS. Strongest counterargument:** The warning points the right way but is dangerously understated as a "may." A sun glitter is not a diffuse texture at bed distance: it is the virtual image of the sun in a wavy mirror — located above/behind the surface (convex-facet demagnification), extended along the glitter path toward the observer, anisotropic, polarised, and moving at wave-slope speed, with per-facet image distances spanning metres. No single bed-distance field, and not even one extra scalar "glitter distance layer," captures a depth distribution + angular sun extent + Fresnel/polarisation weighting. Reusing the bed PSF additionally risks double-counting settled fact (a): sun-disc quadrature Q=25 already integrated the 0.00395 rad solar disc inside transport — convolving the glitter again with a bed-defocus kernel double-integrates the sun. Glitter also saturates (HDR/blooming/veiling glare), which the linear recording model does not own. **Failure condition:** low-sun oblique shot with a glitter path: bed-focused render using the bed distance field smears the glitter into multi-CoC blobs or, conversely, glitter-focused render softens filaments — measured glitter elongation/brightness vs bed filament contrast cannot be fit by any single `s(x,y)`; any HDR frame where glitter clips while filaments do not breaks the shared-field assumption.

**C9 — VERDICT: WEAKENED. Strongest counterargument:** "Harmless overhead" is asserted, not bounded. Caustics exist precisely because the surface is tilted/curved: even with the camera at nadir, local incidence at the refracting facet is routinely 10–20° off-normal, and oblique astigmatism grows with tan²θ — a 20° facet already splits sagittal/tangential virtual-image distances by ~10% of depth, comparable to the f/2.8 DoF budget (±6–7 cm at 1.0 m). Filaments are orientation-selective, so the split does not blur isotropically; it kills contrast along one filament direction while sparing the orthogonal one — exactly the signature a "representative value" convolution cannot reproduce. No tolerance, no θ_max, no preset-specific bound is given, so "main scenes are overhead" is a slogan, not a scope certificate. **Failure condition:** measure/ray-trace sagittal vs tangential apparent depths for the main-preset wave state: if `|s_sag−s_tan| > CoC-derived DoF/2` at the working aperture, or filament contrast drops >20% along one orientation relative to the representative-depth prediction, the simplification is visibly wrong even at nadir; any oblique verification frame fails outright.

## Q1 — frozen E=2.2 / A=2.6 under a real exposure/recording model (attack BOTH branches; no decision)

**Branch 1 — REINTERPRET (keep the numbers as the camera's exposure behaviour). What breaks:** (i) Per-path gain has no camera counterpart. No shutter/aperture/gain/QE(λ)/vignetting acts on "direct only" while sparing ambient — photons are photons at the sensor. Forcing E=2.2 (direct-only) into exposure implies a non-physical, path-tagged gain; any spectral extension (v4-4.5 spectral sensitivity) then inherits a wavelength-independent fudge multiplying only caustic-bearing light, corrupting colour balance of filaments vs background. (ii) Ambient scale A=2.6 as "exposure outcome" disguises a transport/sky deficit as camera speed: it rescales large-area veiling light that real exposure would scale together with direct, breaking linearity tests (grey-wedge tracking) and white-point stability across presets. (iii) Downstream contamination: PJ9 thresholds, loop tone behaviour, and every preset approval tuned under frozen values become camera-dependent without a radiometric anchor — changing body/lens (QE, OLPF) retroactively rewrites scene physics. **What distinguishes it (evidence, not decision):** shoot/render a calibrated diffuse step wedge + flat-water analytic case through the candidate camera model with E/A relabelled as exposure: linear raw tracking (slope = exposure, intercept = black/flare) must hold for direct and ambient jointly; selective direct-only slope ≠ ambient slope falsifies REINTERPRET. Spectral test: narrowband response must scale both terms equally or the branch fails.

**Branch 2 — REPLACE (retire E/A; exposure from shutter/aperture/gain). What breaks:** (i) The tuned look evaporates. Every verified preset, the frozen composite, and the 8 s loop grade were approved WITH E/A baked in; retiring them without a measured absolute radiometric chain (sun/sky irradiance in lux, water transmittance, sensor saturation electrons, tone curve) replaces two known fudges with five unknown physical inputs — uncertainty grows, it does not shrink. (ii) Underdetermination: shutter×aperture×gain alone do not set image level absent absolute scene radiance; the design owns no lux meter, no sun/sky absolute calibration, no sensor full-well spec, so "physical exposure" is theatrical — a new free gain with units. (iii) Downstream contamination is worse than Branch 1 in the short term: shadows tuned under A=2.6 go black, highlights tuned under E=2.2 clip or starve, diffraction-at-f/16 (C7) and glitter HDR (C8) hit rails the old grade avoided, forcing re-verification of all three presets and the loop seam. Double-count risk inverts: if any transport code already normalised by E/A-adjacent scaling, stripping them without auditing transport reintroduces the deficit they masked. **What distinguishes it (evidence, not decision):** absolute-radiometry closure — predict raw digital numbers from sun/sky photometry + water optics + stated shutter/aperture/gain/QE for the flat-water case within stated uncertainty WITHOUT refitting a global scale; residual demand for a direct-only or ambient-only multiplier after closure falsifies pure REPLACE and proves a transport term is still missing.

**Attack on the framing itself:** Both branches assume E/A are exposure-like and therefore belong to stage 4. The more physical hypothesis is that neither belongs here at all: A is a stage-2/3 transport correction (missing in-water multiple scattering / sky / bottom-BRDF energy) and E is a creative grade masquerading as radiometry (direct-term "pop" for caustic legibility). Forcing them into a camera model either way commits a category error — it moves scene-physics errors into the sensor and then "verifies" the sensor against corrupted references. A third shape the design never names: freeze E/A as legacy grade factors with explicit provenance (look, not physics), run stage 4 in normalised relative units with its own calibrated gain chain, and carry a separate transport-closure item (re-derive A from scattering/sky, re-derive or drop E via absolute photometry). Without that separation, Q1 is a choice between mislabelling (Branch 1) and amnesia (Branch 2) — and any "decision material" v4-4.5 prepares that does not include grey-wedge linearity + absolute-lux closure + per-term (direct vs ambient) tracking is insufficient evidence for either.

## Missed items (physics / validation / consistency the design never owns)

1. **Pupil-dependent refraction (entrance-pupil light field).** Marginal aperture rays pierce different wave facets than the chief ray. Matters because the water surface is itself a lens: chief-ray `s(x,y)` + 2-D CoC convolution mis-predicts filament topology/intensity at working apertures. Belongs in stage 4's interface to transport (pupil re-trace requirement), else C1/C2 are unenforceable. Test: chief-ray vs full-pupil re-trace delta at f/2.8.
2. **Effective F-number / NA through the refractive plane.** Cone angle compresses/expands across n; submerged-object DoF and photometric `1/N²` falloff differ from air formulas used in C7. Matters for exposure + DoF correctness. Belongs in v4-4.3 (refractive DoF/exposure correction), not PJ9.
3. **Water dispersion `n(λ)` + lens chromatic aberration jointly.** Caustic filaments are spectrally pure streaks; water dispersion already fringes them before the camera adds longitudinal/lateral colour. A panchromatic PSF + one QE curve cannot predict filament rainbows. Belongs in v4-4.3/v4-4.5 (spectral PSF + CFA/demosaic chain) with a prismatic-filament validation target.
4. **Polarisation.** Fresnel transmission/reflection at the wavy surface is strongly polarising (glitter vs bed contrast, Brewster-adjacent oblique views). Sensor sees polarised light the unpolarised transport ignores. Belongs in transport-to-camera handoff (Stokes or s/p split) or explicitly scoped out with a depolarisation bound; otherwise glitter photometry (C8) is wrong by tens of percent.
5. **Flare / veiling glare / ghosting from glitter.** Bright sun glitter + near-clip filaments pump veiling glare that lifts blacks across the contrast preset. The linear MTF-product + tone path has no glare term. Belongs in v4-4.3/v4-4.5 (glare-spread function + black-level/flare subtraction) with a glitter-out-of-field glare test.
6. **Vignetting + relative illumination + pixel chief-ray-angle (microlens) falloff.** Wide sub preset + short focal lengths lose corners radiometrically and colorimetrically; `W=W_s·(s/f−1)` mapping without distortion/vignetting breaks preset back-computation at edges. Belongs in v4-4.1/v4-4.5 (distortion + flat-field calibration).
7. **Sensor spectral chain: IR/UV cut, CFA, demosaicing, white balance.** Caustic spectra + sky-rich ambient metamerise differently through a Bayer array; demosaic interacts with filament-frequency aliasing. Matters because "conserve total light on flat regions" is necessary but not sufficient — colour conservation is untested. Belongs in v4-4.5 with a submerged colour-checker validation.
8. **Sampling/aliasing audit: transport grid vs pixel aperture sinc vs OLPF vs demosaic vs delivery downscale.** The MTF-product composition assumes a shift-invariant linear chain; per-pixel spatially varying defocus + nonlinear tone + 2048²→delivery resampling violate it. 23 px blur claims (C7) mean nothing absent anti-alias proof. Belongs in v4-4.5 (end-to-end MTF/sampling budget, Nyquist test patterns through water).
9. **Temporal sampling: shutter time vs wave/caustic dynamics vs 8 s loop.** Finite exposure integrates a moving apparent-distance distribution (motion-blurred defocus), not one `s(x,y)`; caustic flicker aliases against frame rate; rolling shutter shears filaments; `s_f(t)` trajectory sampling must respect loop-derivative continuity. Belongs in v4-4.4/v4-4.5 (exposure-time integration + frame-rate aliasing + loop-seam derivative test).
10. **Noise floor + HDR headroom.** Photon shot + read + fixed-pattern noise set the legibility floor for faint filaments exactly where f/16 diffraction (C7) already starves contrast; glitter HDR needs multi-stop headroom/blooming model. Without a noise/DNR + saturation spec, "bed enters at f/16" and glitter-layer claims are untestable. Belongs in v4-4.5 (noise + full-well/clip model).
11. **Occlusion/parallax at finite aperture (3-D bed + objects).** Stage 2 promises 3-D objects; a finite camera sees around relief differently per pupil point — single-view depth cannot encode disocclusion. Matters for oblique/relief scenes where C1's "breaks for 3-D bottom" is load-bearing. Belongs at the v4-4.1/transport boundary (layered/light-field or explicit relief-tolerance bound).
12. **Focus-threshold metrology + loop-closure calibration suite (missing validation).** No owned tests for: orthogonal/infinite-distance reduction, flat-water analytic focus check, energy conservation under varying N/`s_f` on flat regions, preset frame→camera inversion uniqueness, through-focus MTF at filament frequencies, loop-seam (`s_f`, magnification, exposure) continuity. Without these, C4/C6/C7 verifiability claims are promissory. Belongs as a stage-4 acceptance suite, not scattered prose.
13. **Double-counting audit across the full chain.** Settled facts list five forbidden re-applications, but the design adds three new risks: sun-disc quadrature (Q=25) vs finite-aperture glitter integration (C8); deposit-filter σ0.75px vs pixel-aperture sinc vs diffraction stacking; scattering blur (stage 3) vs defocus blur commutativity in turbid water. Each needs a conservation/linearity proof. Belongs as a standing stage-4 entry criterion.
