CLAIMS — for EACH, give a verdict: STANDS / WEAKENED / WRONG, plus the strongest counterargument you can build and a concrete failure condition (a scene, parameter regime, or measurement under which the claim breaks):
C1. Defocus is computed only as an aperture integral over the post-transport radiance field; it is never substituted by pre-blurring the C field or the displacement field. Such a substitution coincides only accidentally for a fronto-parallel, uniform-depth scene, and breaks down for a three-dimensional bottom and oblique viewing.
C2. Focus on the bed is set on the apparent image, not on the geometric distance. Using the geometric distance misses the focal plane by the factor n even in the paraxial case; and obliquely "the image of the bed is not even a surface", so focus cannot be defined without the apparent-distance field.
C3. The frozen composite's E=2.2 and A=2.6 are recording-side constants. A stage-4 exposure/recording model cannot be stacked on top of them; adopting one requires either replacing them or reinterpreting them (the decision to unfreeze is the user's).
C4. A thin lens + geometric blur circle + diffraction is sufficient for the v1 camera; the absence of real-lens aberrations stays within an error range measurable by PSF-shape verification.
C5. The circle of confusion (e.g. d/1730) is a convention of viewing conditions, not physics. Stage 4 outputs the blur-circle diameter itself, and the in-focus threshold is separated out as a display-side parameter (on the PJ9 side).
C6. Focus breathing is a lens-specific change in effective focal length and cannot be derived from a thin lens. v1 excludes it (or accepts it only as an external f_eff(s_f) table).
C7. Depth of field and focus pull exist only in part of the parameter regime. Representative calculation behind this claim (full frame, circle of confusion 0.029 mm, water depth 0.6 m so the bed's apparent depth is 0.6/1.333 = 0.45 m): camera height 1.0 m, 35 mm, f/2.8, focused on the surface — in-focus range about 0.94-1.07 m, and the bed (apparent 1.45 m) has a blur circle of about 0.139 mm, roughly 4.8x the circle of confusion, about 23 px at a 5.9 um pixel pitch; at f/8 the range is about 0.85-1.23 m (bed still out); at f/16 the range is about 0.73-1.59 m and the bed enters, but the Airy disc is about 21 um (about 3.6 px); at camera height 3.0 m, 35 mm, f/8, the range is about 1.92-6.90 m — surface and bed are both in focus and a focus pull is invisible. Therefore an implementation without the regime map is a function that does nothing under most conditions, and shipping the map is mandatory.
C8. Glitter (the specular reflection image of the sun) may not be treatable with the same distance field as the bed pattern. The image distance of a reflection matches neither the bed nor the water surface, so a separate layer may be needed. (This claim is presented as unverified.)
C9. At oblique viewing angles, astigmatism of the apparent image splits "bed focus" in a direction-dependent way. v1 handles it with a representative value of the distance field and declares the split out of scope — and this simplification is harmless in the work's main scenes (capture from nearly directly overhead).
