---

C1. STANDS (narrowly). Strongest counter: An aperture integral over the post-transport radiance is formally correct only if the transport already delivers the correct radiance field at the entrance pupil for every ray that will later be integrated. When the bed is three-dimensional and the viewing is oblique, rays that sample different parts of the aperture may already have undergone different refraction histories and different multiple-scattering paths inside the volume; the "post-transport radiance field" is therefore not a single scalar image that can be simply convolved. The claim's own caveat ("coincides only accidentally for a fronto-parallel, uniform-depth scene") is therefore the normal case, not the exception, once the water surface is free and the bed has topography. Failure condition: Any scene in which the caustic web is formed by rays that cross the free surface at angles >15–20° from the surface normal (the contrast preset or any non-nadir view of the main preset). The resulting PSF will be both spatially variant and directionally anisotropic in a way that a single post-transport integral cannot capture without an additional ray-traced aperture sample set that re-enters the volume.

C2. WEAKENED. Strongest counter: "The image of the bed is not even a surface" is true only for the oblique, non-paraxial regime. In the project's dominant capture geometry (nearly overhead, main and contrast presets) the apparent-depth map is still a smooth, single-valued scalar field to within a few percent; the geometric-distance error is essentially a constant scale factor n. Setting focus on geometric distance and then applying a global n-correction recovers the correct focal plane for all practical purposes. The full per-pixel apparent-distance field is therefore over-specified for the work's own PRESETS. Failure condition: Any capture whose optical axis is >30° from the surface normal and whose depth range spans more than ~0.5 m of real water. In that regime the sagittal and tangential foci separate by more than the depth of field at f/2.8–f/5.6, and a single scalar focus distance becomes meaningless.

C3. STANDS. Strongest counter: None of physical substance. The numbers E = 2.2 and A = 2.6 were frozen before any camera model existed; they are therefore pure look-development constants. Treating them as "recording-side" after the fact is a post-hoc rationalisation. The only coherent positions are the two branches of Q1. Failure condition: Any attempt to keep both the frozen constants and a physically derived exposure equation simultaneously active on the same frame. The resulting double application of gain will be immediately visible as a 2.2× (or 2.6×) brightness jump relative to the settled composite.

C4. WEAKENED. Strongest counter: Thin-lens + geometric CoC + Airy is a first-order model whose residual error is not guaranteed to lie inside a measurable PSF-shape tolerance once the entrance pupil is finite and the object field contains high-spatial-frequency caustic structure. Field curvature alone shifts the best focus by several tens of micrometres across a full-frame sensor at 35 mm; that shift is comparable to the geometric CoC at f/2.8–f/4 for the main preset. Spherical aberration and coma further broaden the core of the PSF in a non-Gaussian way that a pure Airy + geometric circle cannot reproduce. Failure condition: Side-by-side comparison of a real 35 mm / f/2.8 photograph of a high-contrast caustic web (or a high-resolution ray-traced reference with a measured lens model) against the thin-lens prediction; the residual will exceed 5–10 % of peak intensity in the first diffraction ring and will be spatially variant across the frame.

C5. STANDS. Strongest counter: The numerical value 1/1730 (or any other) is indeed a viewing-condition convention, not a physical constant of the lens. Exporting the raw blur-circle diameter (or the full PSF) and leaving the "in-focus" threshold to a later display/eye stage is the only position consistent with the separation of camera from PJ9. Failure condition: Any pipeline that hard-codes a CoC threshold inside stage 4 and then claims the output is "physically focused." The same numerical diameter will be judged sharp or soft depending on print size, viewing distance, and observer CSF—none of which belong to the camera.

C6. STANDS. Strongest counter: Focus breathing is a consequence of the real lens's focal length changing with focus group motion. A pure thin-lens model has no such degree of freedom; any breathing must be injected as an external table. Declaring it out of scope for v1 is therefore consistent. Failure condition: A focus-pull sequence in which the frame boundaries of the caustic web visibly expand or contract while the nominal focal length is held constant. If the artwork's 8 s loop exhibits that motion, the thin-lens model is insufficient and the external table becomes mandatory.

C7. STANDS (and is under-stated). Strongest counter: The representative numbers already show that for the majority of the useful parameter space (camera height ≳ 2 m, N ≳ 5.6, or any longer focal length) the entire water column from surface to bed lies inside the depth of field. Shipping a focus-pull mechanism without an explicit regime map therefore ships a no-op for most of the design space the user will actually explore. Failure condition: Any automated parameter sweep or artist "focus-pull" preset that does not first query the regime map; the resulting animation will be visually identical to a fixed-focus frame for every combination outside the narrow band illustrated in the claim.

C8. WEAKENED (but the caution is correct). Strongest counter: Specular glitter is the image of the solar disc formed by reflection at the free surface. Its virtual distance is therefore the mirror distance of the sun, which for a nearly horizontal surface is essentially at infinity. Treating it with the same apparent-distance field that is used for the refracted bed is therefore a category error. A separate, infinite-distance layer (or a pure directional convolution with the solar disc) is required. The claim's own "may not" is already the correct diagnosis. Failure condition: Any frame in which the glitter pattern is sharp while the bed is intentionally defocused, or vice versa. A single distance field cannot produce both behaviours simultaneously.

C9. WEAKENED. Strongest counter: "Nearly directly overhead" is true for the main and contrast presets, but the sub preset (4 m depth, larger window) already admits non-negligible field angles at the frame edges. Even a few degrees of obliquity produces measurable sagittal/tangential focus split for the high-spatial-frequency caustic web. Declaring the split "out of scope" is therefore an aesthetic decision, not a physics one; the error is present in the very frames used for verification. Failure condition: Measurement of the MTF of a high-contrast caustic edge at the corner of the sub-preset frame under focused conditions; the directional MTF will differ by more than the tolerance set by the diffraction floor.

Q1 – both branches attacked

Branch 1 (REINTERPRET): Keep the numerical values and re-label them as the outcome of the new recording model. What breaks: The numbers cease to be free parameters and become derived quantities. Any subsequent change to shutter, aperture or ISO must still reproduce exactly E = 2.2 and A = 2.6, otherwise the settled composite is altered. The recording model is therefore strait-jacketed by the old look-development constants; it can no longer be used to explore physically different exposures. Contamination: Every future frame inherits an arbitrary brightness offset that has no physical justification once a real camera model exists. Downstream colour grading or PJ9 adaptation stages will treat the offset as "scene-referred," which it is not. Distinguishing evidence: Change shutter time by 1 stop while holding everything else fixed; if the output brightness does not change by 1 stop, the constants are still acting as free gains and the reinterpretation is false.

Branch 2 (REPLACE): Retire E and A; let the recording model compute exposure from shutter × aperture × gain. What breaks: The entire body of reference imagery and the three PRESETS were generated under the frozen composite. Replacing the constants immediately invalidates every previous visual acceptance test. The project loses its only quantitative baseline for "does the image still look like the settled artwork." Contamination: Any residual look-development that was absorbed into E and A (soft ambient lift, slight over-exposure of the direct term, etc.) disappears. The new physically correct exposure will look darker or flatter until the user re-introduces equivalent artistic controls under a different name. Distinguishing evidence: Render the identical camera parameters with the old frozen pipeline and with the new recording model; the pixel-value histograms must match to within the quantisation noise of the 2048² master, otherwise the replacement has changed the artwork.

Framing attack: Both branches assume that the frozen constants must be either reinterpreted or replaced. A third possibility exists and is cleaner: treat E and A as pre-camera artistic multipliers that live outside the physical recording model entirely (analogous to a grade node that is applied after the camera RAW has been formed). The recording model then remains purely physical, the settled look is preserved by an explicit post-process, and neither reinterpretation nor forced replacement is required. The current framing of Q1 hides this option.

Missed items

1. Entrance-pupil ray bundle vs. single central ray. Capture geometry is defined by a per-pixel ray vector, but a finite aperture requires a bundle of rays through the pupil. When the free surface is close (main preset, 0.6 m) the refraction angle changes measurably across a 35 mm pupil; the single-ray approximation already used for transport is no longer sufficient once the aperture integral is performed. Belongs in v4-4.1 / v4-4.3; cannot be deferred.

2. Sensor-plane coordinate mapping under finite conjugate. The design states W = W_s (s/f – 1) but never specifies how the non-uniform magnification that arises from a tilted or non-planar apparent image is resampled onto the rectangular pixel grid. Caustic spatial frequency will be anisotropically stretched. Belongs in v4-4.1 or v4-4.5.

3. Temporal integration during shutter open. The work is an 8 s seamless loop. Any finite shutter time integrates the moving water surface and the moving caustic web. The design's recording model mentions shutter time only as an exposure scalar; it never integrates the radiance field over the open interval. Belongs in v4-4.5; without it the motion blur of glitter and caustic edges is physically wrong.

4. Polarisation state at the lens. Fresnel reflection and refraction at the free surface are strongly polarisation-dependent. A real lens (even a thin one) has coating and glass transmission that further modify the polarisation. The current pipeline is scalar. Ignoring polarisation changes both the amplitude of glitter and the residual polarisation of the transmitted caustic light that reaches the sensor. Belongs in transport or as a minimal Jones-matrix stage inside v4-4.3; currently nowhere.

5. Validation against a known optical bench. The design never requires a quantitative comparison of the computed PSF (or of the rendered caustic edge) against either a measured real-lens PSF or a high-order ray-trace of the same lens. Without that check, claims C4 and C9 remain untested assertions. Belongs as an acceptance test for the whole of stage 4.

6. Consistency of the three PRESETS under the new camera model. The PRESETS are defined as fixed windows at fixed depths. Once a finite camera is introduced, those windows must be re-derived from (position, attitude, f, sensor size). The design states that they "must be back-computable," but never requires that the same numerical images be recovered when the new camera is set to the equivalent parameters. A silent change in perspective or magnification will invalidate every prior visual regression test. Belongs as a hard consistency check inside v4-4.1.

Final verdict on the decomposition: FALLS. v4-4.2 (apparent-distance field) and v4-4.3 (lens imaging) are entangled by pupil refraction and by the non-planar, astigmatic nature of the refracted image; they cannot be cleanly sequenced as independent sub-stages. The recording model (v4-4.5) is forced to confront the frozen constants before the optical model is even finished. The correct shape is a single camera stage that simultaneously owns capture geometry, the refracted object field, the aperture integral, and the sensor sampling, with an explicit regime map and a quantitative PSF validation gate.
